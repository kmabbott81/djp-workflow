# 12d — Editorial Calendar: Pinterest

Two hundred pin ideas for the Anchor ecosystem, distributed evenly across all ten content themes and mapped to an 11-board architecture, with fresh-pin variant rules and a type mix tuned to Pinterest's printable/checklist/infographic intent.
Every pin teaches exactly one evidence-based concept or an honest product angle; every claim traces to the citation registry in [00 — Evidence Foundation](00-evidence-foundation.md) §4, and "what the evidence doesn't show" pins are part of the plan, not an accident.
**All keyword phrases in this file are search hypotheses to be validated in Pinterest Trends before batch production — no search-volume numbers appear anywhere here because we have not measured any.**

---

## Tag legend (recap of foundation §9)

- Format: `[THEME|Tier]` — the tier grades the underlying claim being taught, per the §4 registry.
- Themes: **EF** executive function · **MO** money · **PL** planning · **HO** home · **HA** habits · **TB** time blindness · **DF** decision fatigue · **OR** organization · **PW** product walkthrough · **RE** research explanation.
- Tiers: **T1** clinically supported (adult-ADHD RCTs/meta-analyses) · **T2** moderately supported (package-level or indirect) · **T3** experimental (mechanism-plausible, unproven) · **T4** unsupported/refuted.
- Conventions used below: problem-documentation claims (§4D and the problem-construct rows of §4E) are tagged **T2** — replicated observational evidence that proves problems, not tools. A **T4** tag marks honest "this claim/strategy is unsupported or refuted" content; the debunked claim is the *subject*, and nothing T4 is ever presented as beneficial (§3, §10).
- RE pins (and several others) name their §4 citation key directly in the title line.

## Pin production guidance

**Type mix (target across the pool):** ~40% checklist and printable-preview pins, ~30% infographics, ~15% worksheet/step-card pins, ~10% quote-style insight pins (plain statements, never hype), ~5% short video walkthrough pins (mostly PW). Pinterest intent skews heavily toward printable/checklist/infographic formats, so concept pins should look like tools, not essays.

**Destinations:** concept and research pins → the matching blog post (12a pool), which carries the full citations; printable previews → the free-printables library or First Action Kit opt-in; PW pins → the listing or landing page, Evidence Notes visible. A pin may never claim more than its destination page substantiates (11 §5.5).

**Statistic rule:** any pin that shows a number must show its caveat on the same image (11 §5.5) — e.g., "d = 0.85 (self-guided digital CBT — self-report outcomes, sponsor conflict)."

**Fresh-pin variants (honest repetition):** each idea below supports 2–4 "fresh" pins over the year — a new image template plus an alternate title angle (question form / list-count form / plain-statement form). Variants must keep identical claim strength: rewording may never escalate certainty, and "experimental" stays "experimental" in every variant. Space variants ≥1 week apart; all variants point to the same destination. Example, pin 81: "How long habits really take: 18–254 days (median 66)" → variant "Still waiting for day 21 to feel automatic? The study said median 66 days." Both honest; neither promises.

**Seasonal lead time:** schedule seasonal pins 6–8 weeks ahead of their month in the 12-month arc (see [12 — index](12-editorial-calendar.md) §c) — an operating practice to validate against our own analytics, not a platform fact.
