# 13 — Ethical Marketing Framework

**Executive summary.** This document expands [00 — Evidence Foundation](00-evidence-foundation.md) §10 into the complete rulebook for every public word we publish: listings, sales pages, emails, social posts, and in-product copy. The core position: we sell to a population the market has already burned — a systematic review found 109 ADHD apps and not one with proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020] — and a population for whom financial distress is not an inconvenience but a documented danger [Swedish-Registry]. So our claims ladder is strict (tier-labeled phrasings only), our banned list is explicit, and our conversion strategy is specificity and recognition rather than promises we have not earned. This framework also defines safeguards against exploiting the very impulsivity our products exist to support, a testimonial policy, and a claim-review workflow. It is a good-practice policy document, not legal advice; counsel review is recommended before launch and annually.

---

## 1. The stakes: marketing to a burned, high-stakes audience

Four facts from the foundation set the bar:

1. **The market's claims are noise.** Of 109 ADHD apps reviewed systematically, none provided proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020]. Our buyers have very likely already purchased something "ADHD-proven" that wasn't. Every unearned claim we make is indistinguishable from the noise that burned them; every honest label is a detectable difference.
2. **Money promises are not ordinary marketing risk.** In registry data covering 11.55 million Swedish adults, default rates among adults with ADHD grow steeply into middle age, and financial distress is associated with roughly four times the suicide risk; among men with ADHD who died by suicide, debt rose in the three years prior [Swedish-Registry]. We cite this not for drama but for duty of care: a broken savings promise here can land on someone whose relationship with money is already dangerous. Money-outcome promises are therefore held to the strictest standard in this framework — currently, that means we make none.
3. **Our audience is measurably more exposed to impulse-driven purchase regret.** Adults with ADHD show more impulsive buying and weaker deferral of gratification [Bangma-2019], [Einarsson-2024] and steeper delay discounting [DelayDiscounting-Meta]. Urgency mechanics work *better* on our audience — which is exactly why we refuse them (§5).
4. **Engagement is not evidence.** An app can be adopted, liked, and opened while its primary outcome stays null [FOCUS-2023]. We never dress engagement statistics ("10,000 downloads," "4.9 stars") as proof the product works.

One more, stated as belief rather than evidence: we think verifiable honesty is our only unclonable marketing asset. Anyone can copy a template; nobody can fake a public evidence registry, tier labels on every feature, and a published record of what we got wrong (Part 14 §10).

**The three-readings test.** Every piece of copy must survive three readers: the buyer at the moment of purchase (does it persuade without deceiving?), the same buyer three weeks in, on a hard week (does it still feel true, or does it now read as a broken promise?), and a skeptical regulator or journalist holding it next to our evidence registry (does every claim trace?). Copy that passes only the first reading is a refund, a one-star review, and a reputation debt — deferred, not avoided.

## 2. Claims we CAN make

The tier ladder from foundation §3, expanded into copy-ready phrasings. Rules of use: (1) the claim attaches to the **strategy or method**, never to our product's outcomes; (2) T2/T3 claims carry their qualifier **in the same breath**, not in a footnote; (3) every sales surface carries the disclosure block below.

| Tier | What it covers (examples) | Approved phrasings (copy-paste; adapt only around the bolded spine) |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | The strategy classes with adult-ADHD RCT support: calendar-plus-task-list systems, task decomposition, organization and problem-solving components, weekly review / practice loops, structured digital CBT-derived programs ([Safren-2010], [Solanto-2010], [Matsumoto-2024], [attexis-2026], [Kenter-2023]) | "**Designed around strategies tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD.**" · "**Built on CBT-derived organization and planning methods studied in adults with ADHD.**" · "The weekly review at the heart of this system comes from structured programs tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD — our version is an adaptation, and we label it that way." |
| **T2** | Principles with strong general-population evidence, indirect for ADHD: if-then planning [Gollwitzer-2006], smart defaults [Jachimowicz-2019], cognitive offloading [Offloading-2025], habit timelines [Lally-2010], [Singh-2024] | "**Based on principles studied in general populations** — like if-then planning and smart defaults — **not yet tested in ADHD-specific trials.**" · "If-then planning has a large evidence base in adults generally; ADHD-specific trials of this feature don't exist yet, and we say so." |
| **T3** | Experimental features: body doubling [BodyDoubling-HCI], visual timers [Hallez-2024], purchase cool-downs (mechanism via [DelayDiscounting-Meta]), AI/chat layers [Selaskowski-2023] | "**Experimental — community-endorsed and mechanism-plausible, but unproven.** It's optional, it's labeled, and we'll tell you if the evidence changes." |
| **T4** | Unsupported or refuted approaches | **No marketing statement of benefit, ever.** If a T4 element exists in a product at all, it is labeled experimental and non-punitive in-product and is never a selling point. |

**Rules of use, per tier:**

- **T1 rules.** The claim names the *strategy class* (weekly review, task breakdown, calendar-plus-task system, organization components) — never "this product is proven." T1 claims may appear in headlines. Additional approved variants: "The methods here come from structured skills programs tested with adults with ADHD — we adapted them for a template, and we say exactly where we adapted." · "Organization strategies are among the best-supported components in the adult-ADHD research; that's why they're the spine of this system."
- **T2 rules.** The qualifier travels with the claim — same sentence or the one immediately following, never a footnote. T2 claims may support features but not headline the product. Additional variants: "Here's the honest asterisk: this principle is well studied in adults generally, not yet in ADHD-specific trials." · "Smart defaults have a large general-population evidence base; we use them everywhere a choice can be pre-made."
- **T3 rules.** Always paired with the word "experimental" or "unproven"; always optional in-product; never a purchase driver in paid ads. Additional variant: "The community swears by it. The trials don't exist yet. We ship it labeled, optional, and easy to ignore."
- **T4 rules.** Not marketed, not implied, not illustrated. A T4 mechanic that survives in a product for legacy reasons is labeled experimental and non-punitive in-product and is absent from every sales surface.

**Also always allowed:** describing what the product literally does ("three columns: Now, Next, Later"); describing the documented *problem* ([Bangma-2019], [Barkley-2008], [TimePerception-Review]) with care and without diagnosis-flavored certainty about any individual; stating our honesty practices ("we label every feature's evidence level"); inviting verification ("read our Evidence Notes before you buy").

**Problem-evidence phrasing bank** (fully substantiated; use freely, always about the population, never a promise about the reader):

- "Adults with ADHD carry more debt, save less, and buy more on impulse — that's documented. Tools that fix it aren't. That's why we measure ours." [Bangma-2019], foundation §4D ruling
- "In a 27-year study, adults with ADHD saved 3–4% of income versus 11% for peers." [Barkley-2008]
- "Time blindness is a documented finding, not a character flaw." [TimePerception-Review]
- "Most ADHD apps promise more than the research supports — a systematic review of 109 found none with proof of effectiveness. We decided to compete on the opposite claim." [Pasarelu-2020]
- "Reminders alone don't fix follow-through — a randomized trial showed exactly that. Ours are built differently, and we explain how." [Nordby-2022]

### The mandatory disclosure block (verbatim, reusable)

Place on every sales page, listing, About page, and product start page. Do not reword per-channel; consistency is the point.

> **What this is — and isn't.** Anchor products are self-help organization tools. They are not medical devices, therapy, or treatment for ADHD or any condition, and they don't diagnose anything. They are not a substitute for care from a qualified clinician, for medication, or for therapy. We label the evidence behind every feature in plain language (look for the Evidence Notes); where the evidence is indirect or experimental, we say so.

## 3. Claims we CANNOT make

| Banned claim | Why it's banned | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Clinically proven app/template/system" | No trial of our product exists; the field's 109 reviewed apps had none either [Pasarelu-2020] | "Designed around strategies tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD — the strategies, not this template. Evidence Notes inside." |
| "Treats / cures / fixes / manages your ADHD" | Treatment claims require clinical evidence and a regulatory pathway we have not pursued (Part 14 §9); no product trial | "A place to put the plans, bills, and reminders your working memory shouldn't have to hold." + disclosure block |
| "Guaranteed savings / get out of debt / never miss a bill again" | Foundation §4D ruling: the evidence proves the money *problem*, not any tool; stakes per [Swedish-Registry] | "Built to make the next bill visible and the next transfer small. We haven't measured money outcomes yet — we're measuring, and we'll publish what we find." |
| "Rewires your brain" / "changes your brain chemistry" | No evidence; neuro-halo language banned by §10 | Describe the actual workflow ("one next action, chosen the night before") |
| "Neuroscience-based" / "dopamine hack" (as a halo) | Mechanism talk is not outcome evidence (§6 ruling on neurobiological framing) | Name the actual mechanism honestly at its tier, or say nothing |
| "Build a habit in 21 days" | False; habits take 2–5 months, median 66 days [Singh-2024], [Lally-2010] | "Real habit research says months, not 21 days — so this system is built for the long middle, and for the weeks you miss." |
| "Trains your brain / improves memory & attention through games" | Cognitive training showed no real-world benefit in adults with ADHD [Stern-2016], [Elbe-2023] | We don't ship brain training. Never imply cognitive-training gains. |
| "Reminders that finally make you follow through" | Reminders alone showed no adherence effect in adult-ADHD trials [Nordby-2022]; engagement ≠ outcomes [FOCUS-2023] | "Reminders here do one job: point at a single act you already decided on. Reminders alone don't work — the research is clear, so we designed past it." |
| "AI coach — better than any app/human" | Chatbot showed no superiority over a conventional app [Selaskowski-2023]; [Jang-2021] is mixed and preliminary | "An optional conversational layer. The evidence says the ingredients matter, not the chat window." |
| "Body doubling — proven to work for ADHD" | Survey/HCI evidence only; no RCTs [BodyDoubling-HCI] | "Body doubling is experimental: community-endorsed, mechanism-plausible, unproven. Try it; keep it only if it helps." |
| Before/after money screenshots presented as results | Implies typicality we cannot substantiate; money-promise stakes [Swedish-Registry] | No money before/afters in marketing, period. Customer money stories appear only under the testimonial policy (§6), without numbers in ads. |
| "Only 3 left!" / countdown scarcity on digital goods | False urgency aimed at an impulse-vulnerable audience [Bangma-2019], [Einarsson-2024] | Real deadlines only, stated plainly (§5) |
| "Loved by 10,000 users" as proof it works | Engagement and satisfaction are not outcomes — an app can be adopted and liked while its primary outcome stays null [FOCUS-2023] | Report reach as reach ("10,000 downloads") only when true, never welded to an efficacy implication |
| "Works when nothing else has" / "the last planner you'll ever need" | Unfalsifiable superiority; no head-to-head evidence exists for any tool in this category (foundation §5) | "Different by design, and honest about what's tested — here's the list." |
| "As effective as coaching/therapy" | No comparison data for our products; even strong digital programs weren't superior to active alternatives [Nasri-2023] | Never compare to care. Repeat the disclosure block instead. |
| "Doctor-designed / clinician-approved" (as a halo) | Banned unless literally true, named, and the relationship disclosed (§6.7); an advisory review is not an endorsement of efficacy | If true: "Reviewed by [name], [credential], who advises us — see our advisory disclosures." |

**Images count as claims.** A rising-savings chart implies a savings outcome; brain imagery implies a neurological effect; a serene before/after desk implies a life outcome. Every image on a sales surface passes the same review as the words next to it (§7).

**Absence-of-evidence rule:** if a claim is not traceable to a foundation §4 key at the tier the copy implies, it is banned by default — the burden is on the copy to prove itself to the reviewer (§7), never on the reviewer to prove it wrong.

## 4. Communicating uncertainty without killing conversion

Uncertainty, written well, reads as competence. Five house patterns, then worked examples.

1. **Sell recognition, not outcomes.** Our strongest evidence is *problem* evidence ([Bangma-2019], [Barkley-2008], [TimePerception-Review]). Copy that describes the lived problem with precision ("the bill you remembered at 2am") earns trust no outcome promise can — and it is fully substantiated.
2. **Be concrete about mechanics, humble about results.** We can promise what the product does on screen ("your whole backlog archives in one tap"). We cannot promise what it does in your bank account. Say the first loudly, the second plainly.
3. **Put the label in the same breath.** A qualifier in a footnote is a confession; a qualifier in the sentence is a character trait. "Community-endorsed, unproven, optional" *is* the voice.
4. **Anchor to the strategy's evidence, attribute honestly.** T1 belongs to the method class ([Solanto-2010], [Safren-2010]); our implementation is an adaptation and says so.
5. **Make the honesty inspectable.** Every implied benefit gets an Evidence Note (microformat below). We believe — stated as belief, not evidence — that inspectable honesty converts better with this audience than superlatives; we will test that in our own copy experiments (Part 14 §8 governs which experiments are allowed).

### Evidence Notes microformat (reusable spec)

Four fields, always these four, plain words mandatory. In-product and on-listing versions are identical except length limits.

> **Evidence Note — If-then bill cues**
> **Level:** T2 — moderately supported (indirect for ADHD)
> **In plain words:** If-then planning has a large evidence base in general populations. It hasn't been isolated in ADHD-specific trials.
> **Key(s):** [Gollwitzer-2006]
> **What would upgrade it:** an adult-ADHD trial of this specific feature.

A T3 example, because T3 notes are the ones most tempting to soften:

> **Evidence Note — Body-doubling sessions**
> **Level:** T3 — experimental
> **In plain words:** Community-endorsed and mechanism-plausible; survey and design studies only. No randomized trials yet.
> **Key(s):** [BodyDoubling-HCI]
> **What would upgrade it:** a randomized trial in adults with ADHD with real task-completion outcomes. If one lands, we re-grade publicly (Part 14 §2).

### Approved microcopy bank (recurring lines, pre-cleared)

- "Not a treatment. A place to put things down." — disclosure-adjacent, all surfaces
- "Tested strategies, honestly adapted." — T1 anchor, headline-safe
- "We label every feature's evidence level. All of them." — practice claim, verifiable
- "Missing a week is in the design." — Comeback Mode, T2 ([Lally-2010])
- "Months, not 21 days. Anyone who says otherwise is selling something." — habit honesty ([Singh-2024])
- "Experimental — the community loves it, the trials don't exist yet." — any T3 feature
- "We haven't measured money outcomes yet. We're measuring. We'll publish." — Anchor Money System, all money surfaces
- "Read the Evidence Notes before you buy. We mean it." — listing close

### Six worked copy examples

**1. Hero line — Anchor Money System (sales page)**
- Banned version: "The clinically proven ADHD budget that guarantees you'll finally save."
- Approved: "A money system that assumes you'll forget — so nothing depends on remembering. One bill, one button, one next step. Built on organization methods studied in adults with ADHD; money outcomes not yet measured — we're measuring, and we publish what we find."
- Why it converts: recognition ("assumes you'll forget") does the emotional work the false promise was doing, and the audit trail reads as confidence.

**2. Hero line — Anchor Life OS**
- Banned: "The last productivity system your ADHD brain will ever need. Neuroscience-based."
- Approved: "Your working memory, on a page. Anchor Life OS holds the plans, dates, and half-thoughts your brain keeps dropping — built on CBT-derived planning methods studied in adults with ADHD. Not therapy. A place to put your life down."
- Why it converts: "a place to put your life down" is the offloading mechanism [Offloading-2025] rendered as feeling, with the T1 anchor attached to the method, not the product.

**3. Feature blurb — Comeback Mode**
- Banned: "Never break your streak again!"
- Approved: "You will miss a week. That's in the design. Comeback Mode is one screen and one button: archive the backlog, start from today, no guilt tour. In habit research, missing once didn't derail people — tools that punish misses do that."
- Evidence Note: Level T2 · In plain words: a large habit study found single misses didn't materially harm habit formation; punitive streak designs decay. · Keys: [Lally-2010], [KimCastelli-2021] · Upgrade: an ADHD trial of lapse-recovery design.

**4. Feature blurb — body-doubling sessions (Anchor Lab)**
- Banned: "Body doubling: the proven ADHD productivity hack."
- Approved: "Co-working sessions, honestly labeled: body doubling is experimental — loved by the community, plausible mechanism, no randomized trials yet. Many of us start more easily when someone else is quietly working too. Try two sessions; keep it only if it earns its place."
- Why it converts: permission to disbelieve lowers the wall; "keep it only if it earns its place" makes the buyer the judge.

**5. Etsy listing opener — Anchor Daily Board**
- Banned: "ADHD Planner — Clinically Proven! Fix your focus in 21 days!!"
- Approved: "A daily planner that asks you for three decisions, not thirty. Now, Next, Later — with one pre-decided first step so starting takes two minutes, not an afternoon of setup. Full Evidence Notes included in the download: what's tested in adults with ADHD, what's indirect, what's experimental. Self-help tool, not a treatment."
- Why it converts: the decision-count contrast is the choice-overload insight [IyengarLepper-2000] made visceral, and the disclosure doubles as a differentiator.

**6. Email — First Action Kit welcome (subject + first lines)**
- Banned subject: "This one trick rewires your ADHD brain."
- Approved subject: "Start with two minutes."
- Approved opening: "You don't need a new system today. You need one pre-decided first action — this evening, write tomorrow's single Now task before you close the laptop. If-then planning like this has a large evidence base in general populations (not yet ADHD-specific trials — we're honest about that everywhere). Two minutes. That's the whole assignment."
- Why it converts: a two-minute ask honors Law 2 in the marketing itself; the honesty parenthesis costs one line and buys the relationship.

## 5. Vulnerable-population safeguards

Our buyers are, on average, more prone to impulse purchases they later regret [Bangma-2019], [Einarsson-2024]. A funnel optimized on that fact would work — and would make us the thing we claim to replace. These safeguards are standing policy:

1. **No false urgency.** No countdown timers, no evergreen "sale ends tonight," no fake stock counts on digital goods. **Real deadlines only:** if a launch price genuinely ends, state the date, log it in the claims log (§7), and let it actually end.
2. **Transparent pricing.** Full price visible before any checkout step; renewal terms for Anchor Lab in plain sentences next to the buy button; no drip-revealed fees.
3. **Generous, low-friction refunds.** 30-day no-questions refund on digital products, self-serve where the platform allows, even where platform policy would let us refuse. A refund costs one sale; a regretted purchase costs the customer's trust and our claim to be different.
4. **No shame hooks.** No "stop being lazy," no "your ADHD tax is costing you thousands" fear math, no before/after identity framing.
5. **Calm checkout.** Maximum one optional order bump; no pre-checked boxes — defaults are powerful [Jachimowicz-2019], so we refuse to point them at spending; no post-purchase upsell chains.
6. **A deliberate cooling-off touch.** Every sales page and cart offers "Save this for later" — one click emails the buyer their own link and a one-paragraph summary, no countdown attached. We explicitly accept the conversion cost.

> **Evidence:** T3 (no trial of cooling-off mechanics in a marketing context; mechanism-consistent with [DelayDiscounting-Meta], problem evidence [Bangma-2019], [Einarsson-2024]) · **Confidence:** Low · **Rationale:** steeper delay discounting and elevated impulse buying mean inserting time between urge and purchase should reduce regret purchases in this audience. · **Expected outcome:** fewer refunds and regret purchases, higher long-term trust and repeat purchase; measurably lower same-session conversion. · **Downside:** it costs sales, including from buyers who were genuinely ready; some saved-for-later purchases never happen. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

7. **Money-distress signposting.** Any content touching debt or financial crisis includes a calm pointer to real help (debt-advice charities, crisis lines appropriate to the reader's region) — because of, not despite, [Swedish-Registry]. Our product is never framed as the answer to a financial emergency.

## 6. Testimonial policy

1. **Real people, real purchases.** Testimonials come from verified customers or Anchor Lab members; we keep the receipt trail. No composites, no paid actors presented as customers, no AI-written "reviews."
2. **Written, revocable permission.** Consent on file for the exact quote, name/initial form, and channels; customers can withdraw at any time and we remove within 7 days.
3. **Typicality disclaimer.** Any testimonial describing an outcome carries "Individual experience — results vary; we haven't run outcome studies on this product yet." If we ever have measured typical results, we state those instead — that is the FTC-substantiation spirit (§8).
4. **No clinical-outcome implications.** We decline or edit (with the author's approval) testimonials claiming symptom change ("cured my ADHD," "better than meds"). Gratitude and workflow descriptions: yes. Treatment effects: never.
5. **No money numbers in ads.** A customer may say "I finally have a bill day"; ads never carry "saved $4,000" figures, per §3's before/after ban.
6. **Placeholders always marked.** Mockups and design comps use "Example quote — illustrative, not a real customer" labels that survive into any screenshot. A placeholder that could be mistaken for a real person's endorsement never ships.
7. **Disclose material connections.** Anything from affiliates, partners, or anyone compensated (including free products) is labeled as such.

## 7. Claim-review workflow

**Rule zero:** no public copy without review against the foundation. "Public copy" = listings, sales pages, emails, social posts, ads, in-product marketing surfaces, and this documentation when published.

1. **Writer self-check.** Run checklist items H1–H4 from [06 — ADHD Design Checklist](06-adhd-design-checklist.md), including the banned-language sweep from §3.
2. **Second-person review.** Someone other than the writer (the evidence lead by default) verifies every stated or implied benefit against §4 keys and tier phrasings in §2. Implied claims count: an image of a rising savings chart *is* a savings claim.
3. **Claims log.** Each approved surface gets a log entry: claim → tier → key(s) → approver → date → where published. Real deadlines (§5.1) are logged here too. The log is the audit trail counsel reviews (§8).
4. **Disagreement rule.** Ties break conservative — the stricter tier, the softer claim, per the foundation's prime directive.
5. **Corrections and retractions.** Severity classes:
   - **Class A — material claim wrong** (an outcome or evidence claim that could have influenced purchase): correct all live surfaces within 48 hours, pause affected ads, add a visible correction note where the claim appeared, email purchasers who bought under the claim, log in the annual State of the Evidence report (Part 14 §10).
   - **Class B — tier mislabel or missing qualifier:** fix within 7 days across channels; log.
   - **Class C — typo-level:** fix in the next scheduled update; log optional.
6. **Evidence changes propagate.** When Part 14's re-grading workflow changes a tier, marketing surfaces citing it are swept within 30 days (Part 14 §3). Promotions upgrade copy only after re-review; demotions trigger immediate Class B handling.

## 8. Legal posture (read this, then talk to counsel)

This framework is **good-practice policy, not legal advice**. It is written in the spirit of FTC-style advertising substantiation: have a reasonable basis for claims before publishing; treat health-adjacent claims as requiring competent and reliable evidence (we sidestep this by not making health claims at all); ensure endorsements reflect honest experiences of real users, with typicality handled truthfully; disclose material connections. Marketing wellness products near a clinical condition also has platform-policy dimensions (Etsy, ad networks) with their own claim rules.

**Standing recommendations:** engage counsel to review this framework, the disclosure block, the testimonial policy, and the claims log before launch and annually thereafter; re-review before any paid advertising, any health-adjacent copy change, and any expansion into regulated territory (see Part 14 §9 for the wellness-vs-digital-therapeutic boundary). Nothing in this document creates or substitutes for that review.
