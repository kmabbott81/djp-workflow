# 10 — Email Marketing

> **Status:** Draft for team review · **Owner:** Copywriter / Email Strategist / Behavioral Economist · **Depends on:** [00 — Evidence Foundation](00-evidence-foundation.md) (§4 registry, §7 Ten Laws, §8 vocabulary, §10 marketing rules, §11 metrics) and [09 — Landing Page](09-landing-page.md) (opt-in source, voice) · **Newsletter name:** **The External Brain**.

**Executive summary.** This document specifies the complete Anchor email program: operating principles, the 7-email welcome sequence triggered by the **First Action Kit** (full copy for emails 1–2, detailed outlines for 3–7), a 12-email educational nurture track, a 6-email honest-launch sequence (including a deliberate "should you NOT buy this?" disqualification email), per-product onboarding at days 0/1/3/7/14/30, **The External Brain** weekly newsletter framework with a full example issue, zero-guilt reactivation at 30/60/90 days (full copy at 30), a two-email abandoned-cart flow with a cooling-off nudge (full copy for email 1), a simple segmentation-and-triggers map with deliverability basics, and the compliance-and-care policy. Every email models the product's own laws: one idea, one pre-decided action, honest subject lines, forgiveness by default. Study references in customer-facing copy are plain language annotated with registry keys in inline HTML comments.

## (a) Principles

1. **Educate first, sell second.** Roughly 80% of all sends teach one useful, evidence-grounded concept. Selling is confined to launch windows, quiet product notes (≤20% of a newsletter), and clearly-labeled offer emails.
2. **One idea + one action per email.** Every email is a working demo of Law 2 (one next action) and Law 5 (cue → pre-decided action). If a draft has two CTAs, it's two emails or it's cut. Direct ADHD evidence warns that generic nags do nothing — reminders only work pointed at a specific, small, already-decided act [Nordby-2022] [Gollwitzer-2006].
3. **Honest subject lines.** The subject says what the email is. Banned: fake "re:"/"fwd:", fake personal subjects ("quick question"), false urgency ("FINAL HOURS" without a real, stated deadline), curiosity bait that the body doesn't pay off, guilt bait ("we miss you… 😢").
4. **Frequency caps.** Global cap: **2 marketing emails per week**, all flows combined. Onboarding outranks launch; launch outranks nurture (suppression order in §(i)). Transactional emails (receipts, delivery) are exempt. Max 2 launch campaigns per year list-wide.
5. **Unsubscribe is celebrated, not hidden.** One click, top *and* bottom of every email, honored instantly, followed by a goodbye page that means it: "Inbox space is executive-function space. Well pruned." Down-shift option (monthly-only) offered beside it.
6. **Accessibility and ADHD-friendliness.** Plain-text-first design: real sentences, short lines (~60 characters), one link, minimal images (never image-only), alt text always, no walls of text, generous line spacing in the HTML version. Every email skimmable in 30 seconds; bold carries the skeleton.
7. **Metrics: click-to-action and reply quality — not opens.** Open rates are unreliable since inbox privacy changes (Apple's Mail Privacy Protection and similar prefetch images, inflating opens) — we say this out loud and don't steer by them. We steer by: **click-to-action rate** (unique clicks on the single CTA ÷ delivered), **reply rate and reply quality** (tagged: win / stuck / question / objection), **action-completion proxies** (kit downloaded, Weekly Reset page duplicated), unsubscribe-after-send, and spam complaints (<0.1%). This is Law 10 applied to email: engagement is a cost; function is the goal.

> **Evidence:** T2 [Gollwitzer-2006] [Offloading-2025]; constraints from [Nordby-2022] [FOCUS-2023]; governance Law 10 · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** email is a cue channel, and cues only work when they point at one small pre-decided action; opens measure prefetching, not behavior. · **Expected outcome:** higher per-email action completion; a list that trusts the sender; clean deliverability. · **Downside:** fewer sends and honest subjects will underperform aggressive competitors on short-term revenue. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

**Voice reminder (all emails):** calm friend who has read the research. Grade-7 reading level. Never shame, never hype, never "crush your goals." Sign-off from a named human, not "The Anchor Team."

### Subject-line style guide

The subject *is* the email for most subscribers — many act (or don't) on it alone, and opens can't tell us otherwise (§(a)7). So subjects follow four rules: say what's inside; front-load the concrete noun; ≤55 characters where possible (mobile truncation); sentence case, minimal punctuation, no ALL CAPS, at most one emoji ever (and usually zero).

| Do (ships) | Don't (banned pattern) |
|---|---|
| "Your First Action Kit is here (2 minutes, one win)" | "re: your kit" (fake reply) |
| "Why your last five planners didn't stick" | "You're doing productivity WRONG" (shame + caps) |
| "21 days is a myth. Here's the real number" | "This 1 weird trick rewires ADHD brains" (§10 violations, clickbait) |
| "Launch price ends Friday, May 8, 5pm ET" (only if literally true) | "FINAL HOURS ⏰⏰" (urgency theater) |
| "Please don't buy this if…" | "We miss you 😢" (guilt bait) |
| "Your board is still there. No catching up required." | "Everything you've built is about to be deleted" (false threat) |

Preview text is written by hand for every send (never left to auto-pull), and it extends the subject honestly rather than teasing it.

**Standard footer (every marketing email):**

> You're getting this because you asked for the First Action Kit (or bought an Anchor product).
> **Unsubscribe** in one click — instant, no guilt trip. Or switch to **monthly-only**.
> Anchor makes self-help organization tools — not therapy, not medical care, and not a substitute for either.
> [Company name · physical mailing address]

**Support-resource footer** (appended whenever an email touches money stress or emotional weight — policy in §(j)):

> P.S. If money stress feels bigger than any checklist: **findahelpline.com** lists free, confidential helplines worldwide (US: call/text **988**). Nonprofit credit counselors offer free first sessions. You deserve support, not just a template.

## (b) Welcome sequence — 7 emails / 14 days (trigger: First Action Kit opt-in)

**Arc:** deliver kit + 2-minute first win → why planners fail (mechanism) → capture → one next action → the Weekly Reset → time & money intro → soft offer of Anchor Money System with Evidence Notes. Each email teaches one thing and asks for one tiny act. No selling until email 7, and even then, softly.

> **Evidence:** T1/T2 sequence logic [Safren-2010] [Solanto-2010] [Offloading-2025] [Gollwitzer-2006]; adherence constraint [Kenter-2023] · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** the sequence rebuilds the product's core loop (capture → one action → weekly review) as lived experience before any purchase is proposed; a subscriber who has *done* three tiny actions understands the product better than any sales page could teach. · **Expected outcome:** ≥50% of welcome subscribers complete ≥1 tracked action; email-7 offer converts warm, informed buyers. · **Downside:** 14 days of patience defers revenue; some buyers would have purchased on day 0. · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

### Email 1 — Day 0 — FULL COPY

**Subject:** Your First Action Kit is here (2 minutes, one win)
**Preview text:** No setup. One page. One small thing off your mind.

---

Hi — it's [Name] from Anchor.

Your **First Action Kit** is here:

**→ [Get the First Action Kit]**

Before you save it "for later" (we both know that folder), here's the whole assignment:

**Open page one. Write down the 3 things buzzing in your head right now.** Appointments, that email, the weird car noise. Whatever's circling.

That's it. Two minutes. Don't organize anything.

Why this, first? Because writing things down *outside your head* is one of the best-supported tricks in the research — a meta-analysis found offloading to external reminders reliably improves follow-through, most of all for remembering to do things later. <!-- [Offloading-2025] --> That's the exact thing ADHD brains drop. (That research is from general populations, not ADHD trials — we'll always tell you which is which.)

What happens next: about **one short email every couple of days for two weeks** — each one teaches one idea from the ADHD research and gives you one 2-minute action. After that, one email a week. That's the whole deal.

If it's ever too much: the unsubscribe link below works in one click, instantly. No hard feelings — protecting your attention is kind of our whole thing.

— [Name]

*P.S. Hit reply and tell me: what made you grab the kit? One line is plenty. A human (me) reads every reply.*

[Standard footer]

### Email 2 — Day 2 — FULL COPY

**Subject:** Why your last five planners didn't stick
**Preview text:** It wasn't discipline. It was a design assumption.

---

Hi — [Name] again.

Quick question: how many planners, apps, and systems have you started and dropped?

If the answer embarrasses you, hold on — because it's not the evidence of failure you think it is.

**Every planner you've quit was built on the same hidden assumption: that you'd remember to look at it.**

Regular planners are storage. They assume the *remembering*, the *starting*, and the *choosing what's next* all work fine and just need a container. For ADHD brains, those three are precisely what wobbles. So the planner sits there, politely holding your plans, while life happens somewhere else.

Here's the freeing part: **knowing what to do was never your problem.** In a review of 201 studies, financial education explained about 0.1% of actual money behavior. <!-- [Fernandes-2014] --> Knowledge barely moves behavior — for anyone. Follow-through is a separate machine, and it's the machine ADHD affects.

So the fix isn't a smarter you. It's a system that does three jobs *for* you:

1. **Catches** things the moment they appear (so your head doesn't hold them)
2. **Cues** you at the right moment toward one small act you already chose
3. Shows **one next action** — never the whole terrifying pile

The kit you downloaded is job #1 in miniature. Jobs #2 and #3 are the next two emails.

**Today's 2-minute action:** write down the name of the last system you abandoned and roughly when it died. That's it. You're not journaling — you're collecting data on what didn't fit *you*. It goes on page 2 of the kit.

— [Name]

*One idea per email, one small action. That's on purpose — it's how the whole system works.*

[Standard footer]

### Emails 3–7 — detailed outlines

**Email 3 — Day 4 — Capture: the external brain**
- **Subject:** Your head is a terrible inbox
- **Goal:** install the capture habit (Law 4) with one fixed location.
- **Teach:** working memory is a leaky bucket; the fix isn't holding harder, it's a catch-basin. Offloading research: external reminders reliably improve follow-through, strongest for remembering-to-do-later <!-- [Offloading-2025] --> (general population — labeled as such). Rule: **one** capture spot (The Inbox page in the kit, or any notes app) — capture now, organize never (or later).
- **Single CTA/action:** "Pick your one capture spot and put 5 things in it right now. Ugly is fine."

**Email 4 — Day 6 — One next action (if-then plans)**
- **Subject:** The 10-second sentence that beats willpower
- **Goal:** teach implementation intentions as the engine of follow-through (Laws 2 & 5).
- **Teach:** vague goals stall; "if X, then I do Y" pre-decides the moment. Meta-analysis across 94 tests with over 8,000 people: medium-to-large improvement in follow-through. <!-- [Gollwitzer-2006] --> Honest note: general-population research, mechanism fits ADHD (decision happens *before* the moment of weakness). Template: "If [time/event], then I [tiny action]."
- **Single CTA/action:** "Write one if-then for tomorrow morning. Example: *If I pour coffee, then I look at my capture list.*"

**Email 5 — Day 8 — The Weekly Reset**
- **Subject:** 15 minutes a week (instead of a January overhaul)
- **Goal:** introduce The Weekly Reset as the keystone ritual (Law 6).
- **Teach:** systems don't stay stood up — they get *re*-stood up, weekly, briefly. In a randomized trial of a 12-week organization program for adults with ADHD, the people who did the between-session practice improved most. <!-- [Solanto-2010] --> The Weekly Reset is that practice, shrunk to 15 minutes: empty the Inbox, pick ≤3 priorities, done. Explicitly: skipping a week is normal and recoverable — the ritual is *restartable by design*.
- **Single CTA/action:** "Put a 15-minute block on your calendar for this weekend. The kit's last page is the checklist."

**Email 6 — Day 11 — Time & money: the two leaks**
- **Subject:** The ADHD tax is real (and it isn't your character)
- **Goal:** validate the money problem with evidence; bridge to the money workflow. **Support-resource footer required.**
- **Teach:** time and money are where invisible executive load costs the most. Research: adults with ADHD report more debt, less saving, more impulse buying <!-- [Bangma-2019] -->; one 27-year study found savings of ~3–4% of income vs ~11% in comparison adults <!-- [Barkley-2008] -->. Gently framed: documented pattern, not destiny; the usual "budget harder" advice targets knowledge, and knowledge isn't the leak <!-- [Fernandes-2014] -->. What works instead: one bill, one cue, one pre-decided move.
- **Single CTA/action:** "Find one bill's due date and write one if-then: *If it's the 1st, then I pay just the electric bill.*" (No product pitch in this email. The pitch is email 7; teaching first is the point.)

**Email 7 — Day 14 — The soft offer (Anchor Money System)**
- **Subject:** If money is your heaviest tab, this is the system
- **Goal:** first and only welcome-sequence offer; informed, pressure-free.
- **Structure:** (1) Recap what they've practiced: capture, if-then, reset — "the Money System is those three moves, pre-built for paydays and bills." (2) What it is: stepwise paycheck-and-bills workflow + simple dashboard; Notion and spreadsheet editions; $39. (3) **Evidence Notes, verbatim honesty:** built around strategies tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD <!-- [Safren-2010] [Solanto-2010] -->; *no budgeting tool — ours included — has proven financial outcomes in ADHD trials yet*; every feature carries its evidence tier. (4) 30-day friction-free guarantee. (5) The anti-pressure close: "If now's not the moment, nothing changes — the weekly emails stay free, forever."
- **Single CTA:** "See the Anchor Money System (and its Evidence Notes)."

## (d) Launch sequence — 6 emails (new product), honest mechanics

**Hard rules:** real dates only; if a launch price ends, it ends on a named date and never "returns by popular demand"; no countdown timers in email bodies; no "only 3 left" for digital goods, ever; disqualification email is mandatory in every launch. If there is no real deadline, we say "no deadline — this is just the last email about it."

> **Evidence:** T2/T3 rationale [Einarsson-2024] [DelayDiscounting-Meta]; §10 governance · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** our audience shows documented impulse-buying vulnerability and steeper delay discounting; urgency theater would convert *against* the customer's interest — disqualification and cooling-off convert trust into durable LTV instead. · **Expected outcome:** slightly lower launch-week revenue, materially lower refunds/regret, stronger long-term list. · **Downside:** forgoes proven revenue tactics; requires discipline when numbers dip. · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

**L1 — Announcement (Day 0):** Subject: "New: [Product] — what it is, who it's for." What it does (3 bullets), who it's for / not for (2 bullets each), price, real availability date, link to Evidence Notes. Single CTA: product page. Explicit line: "You'll get at most 5 more emails about this; the last is [date]."

**L2 — Inside look (Day 2):** Subject: "A 4-minute tour of [Product]." Walkthrough (annotated screenshots or 3-min video) of the core loop — e.g., for Anchor Money System: payday → stepwise workflow → one pre-decided transfer. Teach one usable idea even for non-buyers (the if-then bill cue <!-- [Gollwitzer-2006] -->). Single CTA: product page.

**L3 — "Should you NOT buy this?" (Day 4):** Subject: "Please don't buy this if…" Full disqualification list, sincere: (1) your current system works — keep it; (2) you're expecting a treatment — this isn't one, see a clinician first; (3) money is in crisis-mode — free resources first (support footer), template later; (4) it's 1am and this feels urgent — that urge is documented in ADHD research <!-- [Einarsson-2024] -->, and the link works tomorrow at the same price; (5) you hate Notion *and* spreadsheets — nothing here for you yet. Then: who it *is* for (2 lines). Single CTA: "Still a fit? See the details." This email builds more trust than any promise we could write, and it is our impulsive-buyer protection in practice.

**L4 — Evidence & objections (Day 6):** Subject: "What the evidence says (and what we refuse to claim)." The tier table for this product in plain language; the §5 honesty (no head-to-head trials of any planner/budget tool); refuse-to-claim list verbatim from §10; top 3 objections answered (time-to-set-up, Notion, refunds). Single CTA: product page.

**L5 — Stories or maker's-use (Day 8):** Subject: "How [real first name] runs it" — ONLY with real, written-permission stories, typicality disclaimer adjacent (per §10; placeholders never ship). If none exist yet: the maker's own honest week-in-the-life with the product, labeled as exactly that. Single CTA: product page.

**L6 — Honest close (Day 10):** Two variants, chosen by reality: (A) real deadline exists → "Launch price ends [date] at [time, timezone]. After that it's $[X]. That's the whole mechanic — no extensions, no fake re-opens." (B) no deadline → "No countdown here: the product stays available at this price. This is just the last launch email — after today we go back to the weekly notes." Both: 2-line recap, guarantee restated, single CTA. Never a P.S. that re-adds pressure.

## (f) Retention — "The External Brain" weekly newsletter

**Fixed skeleton (every issue, in order):**

1. **One concept** — ≤200 words, one plain-language citation with registry-key comment, honest tier language.
2. **One tiny action** — doable in ≤5 minutes, no purchase required.
3. **One reader win** — real, permissioned, first-name + initial; typicality note in the footer. If no permissioned win is banked, the slot runs a "lab note" (what we're building/measuring) instead — never an invented win.
4. **Product note** — ≤20% of the issue by length, clearly marked "From the shop," at the end, skippable.

Subject format: "The External Brain #N: [concept in plain words]". No clickbait. Same day, same time weekly (consistency is the cue — Law 5).

Write one if-then for this week, on paper or in your Inbox page. One. Format: *If [something that already happens daily], then I [action under 2 minutes].*

## (g) Reactivation — 30/60/90-day lapsed (no product-open or no-click signals)

Zero guilt is the strategy, not a tone choice: lapse-shame is the documented abandonment driver our whole product line is designed against (Law 7).

> **Evidence:** T2 [Lally-2010]; abandonment evidence [Kenter-2023] [FOCUS-2023] · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** a lapse is the modal user journey, not an edge case; the email's job is to make return cost ≈ one tap and to model that missing ≠ failing. · **Expected outcome:** measurable Comeback Rate (return within 14 days of a ≥7-day lapse) from email; reduced list decay. · **Downside:** zero-pressure copy recovers fewer users short-term than discount-bait would. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** Medium

### 30-day email — FULL COPY

**Subject:** Your board is still there. No catching up required.
**Preview text:** One button archives the backlog. That's what it's for.

---

Hi — [Name] here.

Looks like it's been a few weeks since you've been in your Anchor setup. So, first:

**Nothing is wrong.** No streak died (we don't have streaks). Nothing expired. Nobody's disappointed.

Stepping away is the normal path — in one of the best digital-program studies, most people paused somewhere in the middle. <!-- [Kenter-2023] --> And in habit research, breaks didn't undo people's progress; what hurt was the guilt spiral that kept them from coming back. <!-- [Lally-2010] -->

That's exactly why **Comeback Mode** exists. One screen. One button. It archives the old backlog (you're not "behind" — that pile is cancelled), resets Today to empty, and asks you for one single next action.

Not "catch up." Just: one thing.

**→ [Open Comeback Mode]**

And if Anchor's just not it right now? Also fine. The link below drops you to monthly emails, and the one next to it stops them entirely — one click, zero guilt. Your attention is yours.

— [Name]

*P.S. If you're up for it, reply and tell me what pulled you away — a busy season? a snag in the setup? One line helps us build better comebacks.*

[Standard footer]

### 60-day email — outline

- **Subject:** "One thing, not everything."
- **Goal:** shrink the return step below the 30-day version; offer volume downgrade prominently.
- **Structure:** 3 sentences max on forgiveness (no repeat lecture) → single micro-action that works *outside* the product ("write one if-then on paper — that counts as using Anchor") → prominent choice: [Comeback Mode] · [monthly-only emails] · [unsubscribe]. One primary CTA (Comeback Mode); the volume options are quiet text links.

### 90-day email — outline

- **Subject:** "We'll stop emailing weekly (here's where everything lives)."
- **Goal:** respectful auto-pause; preserve the relationship without pressure; list hygiene (§(i)).
- **Structure:** honest statement: "No response in 90 days means the kindest thing is to stop showing up uninvited." → what happens: weekly sends stop automatically; account/templates/kit remain theirs forever at [link]; one annual "still here" note unless they unsubscribe entirely. → single CTA: "Keep the weekly emails" (explicit re-opt-in). No "last chance" framing, no discount bribe. Non-clickers move to dormant (suppressed) status.

## (h) Abandoned cart — 2 emails, no false urgency

Philosophy: an abandoned cart is a *question*, not a leak in a funnel. Our audience's impulse-buying vulnerability [Einarsson-2024] means the ethical flow answers objections, offers "save for later," and actively endorses sleeping on it — we'd rather someone buy tomorrow on purpose than tonight on impulse.

### Cart email 1 — ~20 hours after abandonment — FULL COPY

**Subject:** You left the Anchor Money System in your cart — no rush
**Preview text:** Answers to the usual three questions, and a save-for-later link.

| Lapsed | 61–90 days → reactivation flow |
| Dormant | 90+ days post-reactivation, no signal → suppressed except annual note |

**Trigger map:**

| Trigger | Flow fired | Suppression rules |
|---|---|---|
| Kit opt-in | Welcome 7/14 | Suppresses nurture until complete |
| Any purchase | Onboarding (that product) | Suppresses welcome remainder, nurture, launch, cart — onboarding always wins |
| Cart abandoned | Cart 1–2 | Suppressed if purchased, if onboarding active, or if "save for later" chosen |
| New launch | Launch 1–6 | Owners of that product excluded; Lapsed/Dormant excluded; disqualification email (L3) mandatory |
| 30/60/90-day lapse | Reactivation | All marketing suppressed for Dormant |
| Weekly (default state) | The External Brain | Global cap: ≤2 marketing emails/week/person, enforced platform-level |

**Deliverability basics:** authenticate (SPF, DKIM, DMARC at enforcement); warm any new domain gradually; keep the dormant-suppression policy (sending only to people who act is the single best deliverability lever — and it's also just respect); plain-text-priority templates with low image weight; no link shorteners; every email individually valuable (forwardability is our growth loop); process unsubscribes instantly; monitor spam-complaint rate (<0.1%) and bounce rate (<2%); seed-test major sends. List is opt-in only — no purchased lists, no quiet re-opt-ins, no "we imported you" emails.

**Per-flow success metrics (what each flow is *for* — Law 10 applied):**

| Flow | Primary metric | Secondary | Explicit non-goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome | % completing ≥1 tracked tiny action by day 14 | E7 click-to-offer rate; reply rate on E1 | Open rate |
| Nurture | Click-to-action rate per email; reply quality mix | Unsubscribe-after-send (spike = content miss) | Sends survived |
| Launch | Purchase rate among *clickers of L3/L4* (informed buyers) | Refund rate ≤ target (high refunds = we oversold) | Day-1 revenue spike |
| Onboarding | Day-7 first Weekly Reset completion; day-30 panel opt-in | Day-3 reply (snag) rate | Feature-tour clicks |

| Cart | Question-resolved rate (buy OR save-for-later OR explicit drop) | Reply objections captured | Recovered-revenue-at-any-cost |

**Honest testing policy:** we A/B test subject lines, send times, and structure — never honesty itself. Minimum detectable effect defined before the test; losing variants documented in the swipe file so we don't re-run them; results judged on click-to-action and downstream behavior, never opens.

## (j) Compliance & care

**Legal-spirit basics (not legal advice — counsel reviews all templates before send):**

- **CAN-SPAM:** accurate sender name and subject; physical mailing address in every footer; working unsubscribe honored promptly (law allows 10 business days; we do it instantly); no harvested addresses.
- **GDPR-spirit (applied globally):** explicit opt-in only (the kit form says what they'll get and how often); no pre-checked boxes; data minimization (email + first name + behavioral flags we actually use); export or delete on request; consent records kept; the outcomes panel (§(e) day 30) is separately and explicitly consented.
- **Claims:** every email passes §10 — the same rules as the landing page. Tier-honesty applies in email copy exactly as on the page (T2/T3 concepts are labeled indirect/experimental in-line). Testimonial/reader-win slots ship only with real, written permission and the typicality note; placeholders never send.

**Crisis-content policy (binding):**

1. Any email touching financial distress, overwhelm, or emotional weight (welcome E6, nurture N5, any money-problem education) carries the **support-resource footer** (§(a)).
2. The [Swedish-Registry] suicide association **never appears in marketing email of any kind** — not in subject lines, not in body copy, not as a "did you know" hook. It may appear only in long-form educational content that opens with support resources, and never adjacent to a purchase link.
3. Replies indicating crisis get a humane, non-scripted response pointing to professional resources (findahelpline.com; 988 in the US) — never a product link. Support staff have a written playbook for this.
4. We never buy ads or trigger emails against distress-signal behaviors (e.g., "overdraft" keyword segments). Distress is a reason to help, not a targeting parameter.
