# 01 — Product Vision

> **Status:** Canonical product vision. Every claim below traces to the citation registry in 00 — Evidence Foundation §4; conflict rulings in 00 §6 apply; product names follow 00 §8 verbatim.

**Executive summary.** Adults with ADHD face well-documented, worsening harm in exactly the domains organization tools address — money, time, and follow-through — while the market sells them 109 ADHD-branded apps of which none has published proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020]. Meanwhile, a real evidence base exists: structured organization, planning, and review programs tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD work at the package level, and digital delivery of those same ingredients works too. Anchor exists to translate those tested ingredients into simple, honestly labeled, affordable self-help organization tools — an executive-function prosthetic, not a treatment — designed around the field's true bottleneck: abandonment. We will say plainly what no competitor says: no tool of this kind has ever been trial-tested against a spreadsheet, our own products are untested until we measure them, and success means bills paid and weeks recovered, never minutes in app.

## 1. Why this should exist

### 1.1 The problem is proven

The case for external structure does not rest on marketing anecdotes. It rests on observational and longitudinal evidence that adult ADHD carries measurable, compounding functional costs:

- **Money.** In a registry study of 11.55 million Swedish adults with 189,267 credit records, adults with ADHD entered adulthood with standard credit metrics — and their default rates grew exponentially by middle age. Financial distress was associated with roughly four times the suicide risk, and among men with ADHD who died by suicide, debt had risen in the three years prior [Swedish-Registry]. This is why we treat money tooling as serious work, and why crisis signposting is built in (§3.2).
- **Savings and earnings.** In the 27-year Milwaukee longitudinal study, adults with ADHD saved 3–4% of income versus 11% for controls, with earnings up to roughly 38% lower [Barkley-2008]. The financial gap widens over time [Pelham-2019].
- **Day-to-day financial behavior.** Case-control work shows more debts, less savings-account ownership, more impulsive buying, and lower measured financial competence [Bangma-2019]; impulsive buying and weaker deferral of gratification are independently documented [Einarsson-2024].
- **Mechanism.** Meta-analytic evidence shows steeper delay discounting in ADHD — a stronger measured pull toward the immediate smaller reward [DelayDiscounting-Meta]. This is a cognitive profile, not a character flaw, and it is why "just try harder" tools fail politely.
- **Time.** A decade of research documents deficits in time estimation and reproduction — the construct behind "time blindness" [TimePerception-Review]. That is problem evidence, not license to sell timer gadgets (00 §6 ruling: widgets are T3).

### 1.2 What helps is also known — at the package level

Randomized trials with adults with ADHD support structured, CBT-derived and metacognitive executive-function scaffolding: calendar and task-list systems, task decomposition, time-management training, prioritization, distraction capture, and review loops.

- Group metacognitive therapy teaching time management, organization, and planning produced a treatment-response odds ratio of 5.41 against an active control, and home-exercise completion correlated with improvement [Solanto-2010].
- Structured CBT with a calendar/task-list core produced d = 0.60 on ADHD ratings, with 67% versus 33% responders [Safren-2010].
- Component-level network meta-analysis finds organizational strategies (incremental OR 2.03 for response) and problem-solving techniques (incremental SMD 0.42 for inattention) are the components that carry weight [Matsumoto-2024].
- For work-relevant outcomes — time management, organization, productivity — psychosocial interventions pooled d = 0.56, versus d = 0.19 for medication in that domain [Lauder-2024].
- Digital delivery of the same ingredients works: a fully self-guided digital program produced d = 0.85 on ADHD symptoms at three months in 337 adults [attexis-2026]; a self-guided internet intervention produced d = 0.70 [Kenter-2023]; a coached course teaching adults to structure everyday life with their own smartphones produced clinically significant improvement in 33% versus 0% of controls [LivingSMART-2015].

One caution governs all of it: the unit of proof is the multi-component package, not the isolated feature (00 §3). We translate tested ingredients; we do not claim tested products.

### 1.3 The market selling "solutions" is untrustworthy

A systematic review found 109 ADHD apps on the market — none with proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020]. The same literature refutes much of what those apps sell: brain training does not transfer to real-world gains [Stern-2016], [Elbe-2023]; neurofeedback performs no better than sham [NimmoSmith-2020]; reminder blasts alone do not improve adherence [Nordby-2022]; gamified streaks decay and can go negative over time [KimCastelli-2021]. An honest vendor in this market is not a nice-to-have; it is the differentiator.

### 1.4 The real bottleneck is abandonment

Even in successful trials, only 29% of participants completed all modules [Kenter-2023]. A monitoring app that users adopted at 100% (with incentives) and liked still produced no improvement in its primary outcome, and engagement declined by month three [FOCUS-2023]. Maya's five abandoned planners are the norm, not a personal failure. So the central design problem is not features — it is staying, and above all **returning**. That is why Comeback Mode and the Weekly Reset are the most protected workflows in the ecosystem, and why our signature metric is the Comeback Rate (§6).

### 1.5 The honest gap we occupy

Said out loud, per 00 §5: **no trial has ever tested an ADHD-specific planner or budget tool against Excel, Google Sheets, a printable, or a paper planner.** No isolated feature — visual simplification, today-views, timers, streaks, dashboards — has been validated in adults with ADHD. No financial feature anywhere has been shown to reduce debt, missed bills, or impulse spending in this population. Quality-of-life findings are inconsistent, and durability beyond 12 months is largely unmeasured. Our position in that gap: build from the tested ingredients, label every feature's evidence tier in plain language (Evidence Notes), and measure our own users' functional outcomes (Part 14) instead of borrowing certainty we do not have.

> **Evidence:** T1 for the intervention class we translate ([Solanto-2010], [Safren-2010], [Matsumoto-2024], [Lauder-2024], [attexis-2026], [Kenter-2023]); problem evidence [Swedish-Registry], [Barkley-2008], [Bangma-2019], [DelayDiscounting-Meta]; market failure [Pasarelu-2020]; the products themselves are untested (00 §5) · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** proven need plus proven package ingredients plus an untrustworthy market leaves room for an honest translator. · **Expected outcome:** adults with ADHD get load-reducing structure without hype, and we accumulate real outcome data. · **Downside:** translating packages into self-serve templates may shed active ingredients (coaching, accountability, follow-up), and honesty may convert worse than hype. · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 2. Who it serves

### 2.1 Three personas

**Maya, 32, diagnosed at 29.** Knowledge worker. Five abandoned planners — each abandoned at the moment life got busy, which is when a planner matters most. The evidence says this is typical (29% full completion even in an effective trial [Kenter-2023]). Maya does not need a sixth system to configure; she needs the Anchor Daily Board answering "what now?", a 15-minute Weekly Reset that survives busy weeks, and Comeback Mode so that week three of silence costs her nothing.

**Sam, 41, self-identified.** Has never sought a diagnosis and may never. Avoids the banking app; late fees arrive like weather. Sam's pattern is the documented one — more debt, fewer savings, avoidance-driven costs [Bangma-2019], with stakes that grow over decades [Swedish-Registry]. Sam needs the Anchor Money System's stepwise paycheck/bill workflow, cues that fire one pre-decided act ("8pm: pay one bill" — Law 5), and copy that never once implies laziness.

**Jordan, 26, student and early-career.** Time blindness [TimePerception-Review] and deadline collisions. The closest trial population — college students with ADHD receiving structured CBT plus coaching — showed d = 0.39–1.21 on symptoms and executive functioning [ACCESS-2021]. Jordan needs planning scaffolds that make time visible on the page (Law 9 workflows, T1), decomposition into first actions (Law 2), and honest labels on the timer widgets (T3, experimental).

### 2.2 Diagnosed and self-identified adults — and why that is defensible

We serve both, deliberately:

1. **These are self-help organization tools, not medical care.** No diagnosis is required to buy a planner, and requiring one would be gatekeeping theater, not safety.
2. **The evidence includes self-identified populations.** The MyADHD trial enrolled adults with self-reported ADHD and found d = 0.70 on symptoms [Kenter-2023]; the Living SMART course included subclinical ADHD and produced blind-rated clinically significant improvement in 33% versus 0% [LivingSMART-2015]. Benefit from structured self-help does not appear to require a confirmed diagnosis.
3. **Access to assessment is slow, costly, and uneven** — design rationale, not an evidence claim. Refusing Sam until he produces paperwork serves no one.

**What we do NOT infer from self-identification.** We never confirm, suggest, or imply that anyone has ADHD. We run no quizzes that "detect" ADHD. We never message "if you struggle with this, you probably have ADHD." We make no treatment, medication, or diagnosis recommendations either way. Self-identification is treated as a description of struggles someone wants help organizing around — never as clinical data — and outcomes-panel reporting (Part 14) makes no diagnostic claims about participants.

> **Evidence:** T1 that self-guided digital structure helped self-reported and subclinical populations ([Kenter-2023], [LivingSMART-2015]); the inclusion decision itself is policy grounded in that evidence · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** benefit in trials did not depend on formal diagnosis, and our products carry no medical risk surface that would demand gatekeeping. · **Expected outcome:** Sam-type users get help years before (or without) a diagnosis, without being medicalized by us. · **Downside:** some buyers' difficulties may stem from something a planner cannot help and a clinician could; mitigated by explicit signposting and by never discouraging professional care. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

## 3. Who it does NOT serve

Every "no" below is an ethical feature, not a lost sale. The 109-app market exists precisely because vendors sell to everyone [Pasarelu-2020]. Refusing users we cannot help protects them from mismatched purchases, protects buyers from feature bloat, and protects the only asset this brand has: being believed.

### 3.1 Children and teens

Our evidence base is adults (the core reviews and trials enroll adults 18–65, e.g., [Cochrane-2018]). Child evidence does not transfer cleanly — we cite the visual-timer study [Hallez-2024] only as the children's finding we refuse to stretch to adults, and we hold ourselves to the same standard in reverse. We also cannot meet the safeguarding and privacy obligations serving minors would demand. All products and marketing state: designed for adults, 18 and over.

### 3.2 People in acute crisis

Financial distress in this population is associated with roughly four times the suicide risk [Swedish-Registry]. Our audience overlaps with people in real danger, so every money product and onboarding email carries a plain, warm signpost: if you are in a mental-health or financial crisis, contact local emergency services, a crisis line, or a qualified professional first — the planner can wait, and it will still be here. Organization tools are not crisis care, and pretending otherwise would be the most harmful claim we could make.

### 3.3 People seeking diagnosis, treatment, or medication management

Mandatory disclosure (00 §10): these are self-help organization tools, not medical devices or therapy; they do not replace diagnosis, medication, or therapy. We refuse the medication-management lane entirely — a dedicated monitoring app with financial incentives still failed to improve medication possession ratio [FOCUS-2023], and we will not sell what a purpose-built trial could not deliver.

### 3.4 People who love building complex custom systems

We say this with respect: some people genuinely enjoy elaborate custom dashboards, and they are not our users. Law 3 exists because fewer options outperform more (30% versus 3% purchase in the classic choice-overload experiment [IyengarLepper-2000]) and because configuration is where Maya's five planners died. Tinkerers will feel fenced in by our defaults, ask for the features we deliberately withhold, and be happier elsewhere. Serving them would bloat the product against the users it exists for.

### 3.5 Teams and enterprise

Delegation, approvals, admin, compliance — different jobs, different product, and a guaranteed engine of feature creep. Anchor manages one adult's life. Anchor Home Base supports a shared household checklist; that is the boundary.

> **Evidence:** No direct evidence — scope policy; supporting anchors: adult-only evidence base [Cochrane-2018], crisis-risk overlap [Swedish-Registry], medication-adherence null [FOCUS-2023], choice overload [IyengarLepper-2000] · **Confidence:** High (as policy coherence, not as outcome data) · **Rationale:** every excluded segment either exceeds our evidence, exceeds our duty of care, or would force complexity that harms core users. · **Expected outcome:** fewer mismatched purchases and refunds; preserved trust; a product that stays small. · **Downside:** real revenue declined, and some excluded people will buy anyway — signposting must assume they will. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

## 4. Core philosophy: the prosthetic stance

ADHD is a performance problem at the point of performance, not a knowledge problem. Education alone barely moves behavior — financial education explains about 0.1% of variance in financial behavior and decays within about 20 months [Fernandes-2014]; later estimates are larger but still small, and only action-linked, just-in-time designs do better [KaiserMenkhoff-2020]. What does work is offloading: moving intentions into external systems reliably improves performance, most of all for prospective memory — remembering to do the thing at the time of the thing — which is the core ADHD-relevant deficit [Offloading-2025].

So Anchor is a prosthetic, in the dignified sense of the word: like glasses, it does not train your eyes — it does part of the work so you can do yours. The tool holds the memory, sequences the steps, shrinks the decision, and makes time visible. It never assigns willpower as homework, and it never treats needing support as a deficiency to graduate from. The design consequence is subtraction: fewer features, fewer options, fewer decisions per screen. Where most products earn upgrades by adding, we earn trust by removing.

The Ten Design Laws (00 §7) are the operating constitution. In plain language:

| # | Law | In plain words | Anchor (evidence and tier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Externalize, don't educate | The tool holds the memory and the steps; it doesn't lecture you into remembering | [Fernandes-2014], [Offloading-2025], [Safren-2010] — T1/T2 |
| 2 | One next action | Every screen answers one question: what is the one small thing now? | [Safren-2010], [Solanto-2010], [Matsumoto-2024] — T1 |
| 3 | Reduce decisions | Fewer options, filled-in defaults; setup never becomes a hobby | [Jachimowicz-2019], [IyengarLepper-2000] — T2 indirect |
| 4 | Capture first, organize later | Anything on your mind goes into The Inbox in seconds; filing can wait | [Offloading-2025], [LivingSMART-2015] — T1/T2 |
| 5 | Cue → pre-decided action | A reminder exists only to fire one small act you already chose: "8pm — pay one bill" | [Gollwitzer-2006]; constraints [Nordby-2022], [FOCUS-2023] — T2 with T1 constraints |
| 6 | Practice loops and review rituals | One short Weekly Reset beats daily perfection; the ritual is the engine | [Solanto-2010], [Selaskowski-2022], [NICE-NG87] — T1 |
| 7 | Forgiveness by design | Missing a day or a month breaks nothing; Comeback Mode makes returning one button, never a walk of shame | [Lally-2010], [KimCastelli-2021], [Kenter-2023] — T2 |
| 8 | Automate everything automatable | If the tool can do it without you, it should — the medium limits how much (see 02 §2) | [Jachimowicz-2019], [ThalerBenartzi-2004] with its causal caveat — T2 indirect |
| 9 | Time made visible and scaffolded | Planning workflows put time on the page (tested); timer widgets are optional extras (experimental) | [Solanto-2010], [Lauder-2024]; widgets [Hallez-2024], children — T1 workflow / T3 widget |
| 10 | Measure function, not engagement | We count bills paid and weeks recovered, never minutes in app | [FOCUS-2023], [NimmoSmith-2020], [LopezCampos-2025] — governance |

> **Evidence:** Tiers as tabled above; the laws are the foundation's own synthesis (00 §7) adopted here as binding governance · **Confidence:** High that these are the correct reading of the evidence; Moderate that template-form products can honor all ten (Laws 5 and 8 are medium-limited — see 02) · **Rationale:** ADHD-relevant failure happens at the point of performance, so the product does the executive work rather than teaching it. · **Expected outcome:** lower cognitive load per unit of life managed; fewer abandonments at the configuration stage. · **Downside:** subtraction is commercially unglamorous; some buyers equate more features with more value. · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 5. Core outcomes

If Anchor works, it shows up in a user's Tuesday, not in our analytics. Each core outcome maps to a canonical metric (00 §11):

| Outcome in the user's life | Canonical metric (00 §11) | Why this is the right thing to count |
|---|---|---|
| Sam's bills leave on time | 1 — Bill-payment timeliness (on-time / total, self-logged) | The need is proven, no tool is [Bangma-2019], [Swedish-Registry]; so we measure ourselves rather than promise |
| Late fees and overdrafts get rarer | 2 — Late fees and overdrafts per month (self-logged count) | Direct cost of the documented avoidance pattern [Bangma-2019] |
| Planned savings transfers actually happen | 3 — Savings-transfer completion rate (planned vs executed) | The 3–4% vs 11% savings gap [Barkley-2008]; automation mechanism [ThalerBenartzi-2004], with its causal caveat |
| Small task lists finish instead of haunting | 4 — Task completion rate on the Anchor Daily Board (done / committed, committed ≤3 per day by design) | Decomposition and one-next-action are T1 ingredients [Safren-2010]; organization/time-management behaviors partially mediated symptom gains [Inflow-2026] |
| The Weekly Reset keeps happening, imperfectly | 5 — Weekly Reset completion (target ≥50% of active weeks) | Home-exercise completion correlated with improvement [Solanto-2010] — our strongest defensible leading indicator |
| A lost week stays a lost week, not a lost system | 6 — Retention at weeks 4/12/26 and Comeback Rate: % who return within 14 days after a ≥7-day lapse | One missed occasion does not materially harm habit formation [Lally-2010]; abandonment is the field's failure mode [Kenter-2023], [FOCUS-2023] |
| Life feels more workable | 7 — Optional QoL/functioning self-check-ins (AAQoL/WSAS-style in spirit; self-tracking, not diagnosis) | QoL evidence is mixed ([Cochrane-2018] unclear; [attexis-2026], [Kenter-2023] positive; [Inflow-2026] no functional-impairment change) — so we track it honestly rather than claim it |

Note what is absent: symptom reduction is not a core outcome. Package-level trials moved symptoms; our untested templates get no such presumption. If the outcomes panel (Part 14) ever earns a symptom claim, it will arrive with data attached.

## 6. Success metrics — targets, benchmarks, and non-goals

Restating 00 §11 with working targets. **Targets are business judgments, not evidence**; they will be recalibrated by panel data. Financial metrics use each user's own first four weeks as baseline — we compare people to themselves, not to invented norms.

| # | Metric | Working target | Honest benchmark / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bill-payment timeliness | Improvement vs user's own 4-week baseline | No external tool benchmark exists (00 §5); self-logged, self-selected panel |
| 2 | Late fees and overdrafts per month | Downward trend vs own baseline | Same caveats; count, not currency, to keep logging friction near zero |
| 3 | Savings-transfer completion | ≥70% of planned transfers executed | Aspiration; no ADHD-specific benchmark exists |
| 4 | Daily Board task completion | ≥60% of committed done, with commitment capped at 3/day | The cap is the design: small committed lists, mostly finished, beat long lists mostly haunted |
| 5 | Weekly Reset completion | ≥50% of active weeks (canonical) | Deliberately below perfection — the target itself encodes Law 7 |
| 6 | Retention and recovery | >40% of buyers still using any core workflow at week 12; Comeback Rate ≥50% return within 14 days after a ≥7-day lapse | Field benchmarks to beat: 29% completed all modules, 59% completed ≥5 of 7 [Kenter-2023]; engagement decay by month 3 [FOCUS-2023]. Comeback Rate has no external benchmark — we are defining it |
| 7 | QoL / functioning check-ins | Report distributions honestly; no target | Not a treatment; inconsistent QoL evidence (00 §5) forbids a promised trajectory |

All measurement is voluntary, consented, privacy-respecting, via the outcomes panel (Part 14). Templates and printables cannot phone home — a limitation and, for privacy, a feature.

**Explicit non-goals.** We do not target and will not celebrate: daily active users, session length, notification open rate, or streak length. Engagement is a cost the user pays, not a goal we harvest — an app users liked and opened still failed its primary outcome [FOCUS-2023]; engagement-optimized formats are where the umbrella review found adverse-effect signals [LopezCampos-2025]; streak mechanics decay and can turn negative long-term [KimCastelli-2021]. Time-in-tool should trend **down** per unit of life managed (Law 10). If a user runs their week from one printed page and never opens Notion again, that is a success story, and our metrics must be able to say so.

> **Evidence:** Metric framework per 00 §11; benchmark anchors [Kenter-2023], [FOCUS-2023]; anti-engagement stance [FOCUS-2023], [KimCastelli-2021], [LopezCampos-2025]; Weekly Reset as leading indicator [Solanto-2010] · **Confidence:** High for the framework's honesty; Low for every numeric target until panel data exists · **Rationale:** measure the functions the problem evidence says are broken, against each user's own baseline. · **Expected outcome:** we learn whether our products work — possibly the first vendor in this category able to say either way. · **Downside:** honest metrics can return bad news publicly; voluntary panels self-select toward engaged users, which we must state in every report. · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 7. Positioning

**Positioning statement.** For adults with ADHD — diagnosed or self-identified — who have abandoned more planners than they can count, Anchor is a family of plain, low-decision organization tools that work like an external executive function: they hold what you would otherwise have to remember, shrink every plan to one next action, and expect you to disappear sometimes. Unlike the 109-app market that claims proof it does not have [Pasarelu-2020], every Anchor feature carries a plain-language evidence label — Evidence Notes — and we publish what our users' outcomes actually show.

**The one promise we CAN make:**

> Anchor is built from the organization, planning, and review strategies tested in randomized trials with adults with ADHD — labeled feature by feature with exactly how strong the evidence is, and designed so that a lost week never costs you the system.

That promise is entirely within our control: provenance, labeling, and forgiveness are design facts, not outcome predictions.

**Three promises we refuse to make:**

1. **"This will reduce your ADHD symptoms."** Trials support treatment packages with coaching, homework, and follow-up ([Solanto-2010], [Safren-2010], [attexis-2026]); our templates carry those packages' ingredients but have not been trialed. We are a self-help organization tool, not a treatment — and we say so on the listing.
2. **"You'll save money and never miss another bill."** No financial feature in any product, anywhere, has been shown to reduce debt, missed bills, or impulse spending in adults with ADHD (00 §5). We build the mechanism, label it honestly, and measure — the first outcome claim waits for Part 14 data.
3. **"This beats your spreadsheet — and this time you'll actually stick with it."** No head-to-head trial against a spreadsheet or paper planner exists for any tool in this category, including ours (00 §5), and abandonment is the documented norm even for effective programs [Kenter-2023]. We design for coming back, not for never leaving — Comeback Mode is the honest version of this promise.

> **Evidence:** Language rules per 00 §10; refusals grounded in 00 §5 and [Pasarelu-2020], [Kenter-2023]; the permitted claim uses the sanctioned T1 framing ([Solanto-2010], [Safren-2010], [Matsumoto-2024]) · **Confidence:** High · **Rationale:** in a market of unprovable promises, the defensible position is provenance plus honesty plus forgiveness. · **Expected outcome:** slower but compounding trust; reviews that cite being leveled with as the reason to buy. · **Downside:** hype outsells honesty in the short term, and refused promises hand competitors easy copy. · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High
