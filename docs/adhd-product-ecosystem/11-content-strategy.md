# 11 — Content Strategy

> **Executive summary:** Anchor's content engine is an educational public good that happens to be our top-of-funnel. Every video, post, pin, and email teaches exactly one evidence-based concept about executive function, planning, habits, time, money, or organization — accurately, warmly, and without hype — because in a market where a systematic review found 109 ADHD apps and none with proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020], *our honesty about weak evidence is the moat*. This document defines the ten content pillars (mapped to the foundation's theme codes), the accuracy workflow that keys every factual claim to the canonical registry in [00 — Evidence Foundation](00-evidence-foundation.md) §4, a ten-myth response library with exact honest framings, per-platform strategies for nine channels, a repurposing waterfall and batch schedule designed for an ADHD creator (we practice what we preach), community and moderation policy, Law 10-compliant metrics, and disclosure ethics. Platform tactics below are current best practice to validate — never claimed algorithm facts.

## 1. Mission: an educational engine that makes executive function understandable

**The mission.** Most adults with ADHD have been sold two stories: "you just need more discipline" (shame) or "this one hack rewires your brain" (hype). The research supports neither. It supports something quieter and more hopeful: structured, external scaffolding — calendars, task breakdown, capture, review rituals — taught and practiced, works in randomized trials with adults with ADHD [Cochrane-2018][Safren-2010][Solanto-2010][Knouse-2017]. Our content exists to make that body of evidence understandable, usable, and free. Content is simultaneously:

1. **A public good.** The concepts we teach (one next action, capture first, if-then plans, forgiving habit metrics, weekly review) work on paper, in Google Sheets, or in a competitor's app. We teach them anyway. Someone who never buys anything from us should still leave better equipped.
2. **Our top-of-funnel.** People who learn from us come to trust us; some of them will want the pre-built version of what we taught (the First Action Kit, then Anchor Life OS, Anchor Money System, and eventually the Anchor App).

**The trust thesis.** The ADHD tools market is saturated with unvalidated claims: 109 apps reviewed, 23 of them psychoeducation apps, *none* with proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020]; brain training refuted in adults [Stern-2016][Elbe-2023]; neurofeedback no better than sham [NimmoSmith-2020]; the whole digital-intervention field rated low-quality evidence by an umbrella review [LopezCampos-2025]. Nobody in this market can honestly say "clinically proven." So we win by being the one voice that says out loud what is proven, what is promising, and what is marketing — including about our own products (that is what Evidence Notes are for). Trust compounds; hype decays. Every strategic decision below serves that thesis.

> **Evidence:** T1 for the concepts taught ([Cochrane-2018][Safren-2010][Solanto-2010][Matsumoto-2024]); the trust-as-moat positioning itself is design rationale grounded in the documented absence of validated competitors [Pasarelu-2020][LopezCampos-2025] · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** in a market where no product claim survives scrutiny, verifiable honesty is the only durable differentiator · **Expected outcome:** audience trust that converts to email subscribers and patient, informed customers with realistic expectations · **Downside:** honest content grows slower than hype and can be outcompeted short-term by louder channels · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 2. Content pillars: ten themes mapped to the evidence

The ten pillars are exactly the theme codes defined in foundation §9. Every piece of content is tagged with one pillar code plus the tier of the underlying claim it teaches (e.g., `[EF|T1]`, `[TB|T3]`). One piece teaches one concept — never a "17 tips" grab bag. The one-year editorial calendar built from these pillars and tags lives in [12 — Editorial Calendar](12-editorial-calendar.md); this section defines what each pillar is allowed to promise.

**1. EF — Executive function**
- **Promise:** understand what executive function is, why effort isn't the missing ingredient, and why external scaffolding works.
- **Example angles:** "Externalize, don't educate" explained `[EF|T1]`; what CBT for adult ADHD actually contains [Safren-2010]; why offloading memory to a trusted system beats trying to remember [Offloading-2025]; what "point of performance" means.
- **Citation keys:** [Cochrane-2018][Knouse-2017][Safren-2010][Solanto-2010][Matsumoto-2024][Offloading-2025][Fernandes-2014]
- **Banned angles:** "train your executive function with games" (refuted [Stern-2016][Elbe-2023]); deficit-shaming; confident dopamine neuroscience as outcome proof (§6 ruling).

**2. MO — Money**
- **Promise:** name the well-documented ADHD money problem without shame, and teach structural (not willpower) responses honestly labeled as untested.
- **Example angles:** what a 27-year follow-up found about ADHD and savings [Barkley-2008]; impulsive buying and delay discounting explained [Einarsson-2024][DelayDiscounting-Meta]; why financial literacy courses barely move behavior [Fernandes-2014][KaiserMenkhoff-2020]; the honest state of budgeting tools (§5 of the foundation: none validated).
- **Citation keys:** [Bangma-2019][Barkley-2008][Pelham-2019][Einarsson-2024][DelayDiscounting-Meta][Swedish-Registry][Fernandes-2014][KaiserMenkhoff-2020][ThalerBenartzi-2004]
- **Banned angles:** promising savings/debt outcomes (foundation §4D ruling); using the [Swedish-Registry] suicide-risk finding in hooks or thumbnails (long-form context with crisis resources only, per §7 below); "this app fixes your finances."

**3. PL — Planning**
- **Promise:** planning methods from trials, shrunk to ADHD-friendly size: one next action, task breakdown, if-then plans.
- **Example angles:** how to split a dreaded task into a first action [Safren-2010]; writing an implementation intention in 20 seconds [Gollwitzer-2006]; problem-solving structure as the evidence-backed component [Matsumoto-2024]; the Anchor Daily Board's Now / Next / Later logic `[PL|T1]`.
- **Citation keys:** [Safren-2010][Solanto-2010][Matsumoto-2024][Gollwitzer-2006][Lauder-2024]
- **Banned angles:** "the perfect planner will fix you"; productivity-guru maximalism; planning as a moral virtue.

**4. HO — Home**
- **Promise:** home management with less friction and zero shame — routines, shared checklists, environmental structuring.
- **Example angles:** why checklists are cognitive prosthetics, not crutches [Offloading-2025][LGO]; OT-consensus environmental structuring [UKAAN-2021]; Anchor Home Base walkthroughs of recurring-chore capture `[HO|T2]`.
- **Citation keys:** [UKAAN-2021][NICE-NG87][LGO][Offloading-2025]
- **Banned angles:** cleanfluencer perfection; "lazy" framing; before/after shame theater.

**5. HA — Habits**
- **Promise:** what habit research actually says — realistic timelines, forgiveness, momentum over streaks.
- **Example angles:** 66-day median, 18–254 range [Lally-2010]; habits take 2–5 months [Singh-2024]; why one missed day didn't materially harm formation [Lally-2010]; why Anchor Routines counts weekly momentum instead of streaks [KimCastelli-2021] `[HA|T2]`.
- **Citation keys:** [Lally-2010][Singh-2024][KimCastelli-2021][Gollwitzer-2006]
- **Banned angles:** "21 days"; streak worship; discipline moralizing.

**6. TB — Time blindness**
- **Promise:** time-perception differences are real and measurable; time-management *skills* are trainable; widgets are experimental — we teach the difference.
- **Example angles:** what time-perception studies measure [TimePerception-Review]; time-management training in trials [Solanto-2010][Lauder-2024]; the honest status of visual timers (child evidence only) [Hallez-2024] `[TB|T3]`.
- **Citation keys:** [TimePerception-Review][Solanto-2010][Lauder-2024][Hallez-2024]
- **Banned angles:** "this timer cures time blindness"; presenting the children's timer study as adult proof (§6 ruling).

**7. DF — Decision fatigue**
- **Promise:** fewer, smaller decisions by design — defaults, templates, and option-trimming, honestly sourced from general-population research.
- **Example angles:** the jam study (6 options → 30% purchase vs 24 → 3%) [IyengarLepper-2000]; the default effect (d = 0.68) [Jachimowicz-2019]; how we use defaults inside Anchor Money System `[DF|T2]`.
- **Citation keys:** [IyengarLepper-2000][Jachimowicz-2019]
- **Banned angles:** ego-depletion pop-neuroscience; claiming these general-population findings are ADHD-proven (they are indirect, T2).

**8. OR — Organization**
- **Promise:** organization as a learnable, externalized workflow — the single best-evidenced component in the literature.
- **Example angles:** organisational strategies raised treatment response (incremental OR 2.03) in a component meta-analysis [Matsumoto-2024]; capture-first workflows and The Inbox [Offloading-2025] `[OR|T1]`; what "organized enough" looks like (function, not aesthetics).
- **Citation keys:** [Matsumoto-2024][Solanto-2010][Safren-2010][LGO][UKAAN-2021][Offloading-2025]
- **Banned angles:** aesthetic organization as the goal; rainbow-shelf perfectionism; "if it's not pretty it doesn't count."

**9. PW — Product walkthrough**
- **Promise:** transparent demos of Anchor products with their Evidence Notes visible — tier labels on camera, limitations said out loud.
- **Example angles:** The Weekly Reset in real time (15 minutes, unedited) `[PW|T1]`; Comeback Mode demo — restarting after two weeks away `[PW|T2]`; why the Anchor Daily Board caps commitments at three `[PW|T1]`; "what we don't know yet" segments.
- **Citation keys:** whichever keys back the feature being shown, per foundation §4 and §7; market context [Pasarelu-2020]
- **Banned angles:** outcome promises; fake speed ("set up your whole life in 10 minutes"); hiding the tier label; testimonial-as-proof.

**10. RE — Research explanation**
- **Promise:** plain-language study breakdowns — including nulls and our own products' evidence gaps — so the audience can read past headlines.
- **Example angles:** anatomy of the attexis trial and its caveats [attexis-2026]; why "no better than sham" matters [NimmoSmith-2020]; what mediation means in the Inflow trial [Inflow-2026]; why engagement isn't an outcome [FOCUS-2023]; how to spot an unsupported app claim [Pasarelu-2020].
- **Citation keys:** the full §4 registry; especially [Cochrane-2018][attexis-2026][Kenter-2023][Inflow-2026][Nasri-2023][LopezCampos-2025][Nordby-2022][FOCUS-2023][Stern-2016][Elbe-2023][NimmoSmith-2020][Selaskowski-2023]
- **Banned angles:** "new study proves…" overclaiming; single-study certainty; dunking on researchers; presenting effect sizes without caveats the foundation attaches to them.

> **Evidence:** pillar taxonomy mirrors foundation §9 theme codes; underlying concepts range T1–T3 as tagged per piece · **Confidence:** High (taxonomy), Moderate (audience fit) · **Rationale:** one-concept-per-piece matches both teaching clarity and the reduced-decisions law (Law 3) applied to our own editorial process · **Expected outcome:** every published piece is auditable against §4 by its tag · **Downside:** strict tagging slows production and rules out easy listicle formats · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

## 3. The accuracy workflow

Every piece moves through six gates. Nothing skips gate 3 or 4, ever — not for a trend, not for a launch week.

1. **Idea → The Inbox.** All content ideas are captured to the channel's Inbox (same capture-first system we sell, Law 4) with a pillar code guess. No idea is evaluated at capture time.
2. **Script.** Drafted in our two-column script template: left column = the words; right column = a claim ledger. Every factual sentence gets a row: *claim → §4 citation key → tier → confidence*. A sentence that can't be keyed is either (a) rewritten as clearly labeled opinion/experience ("we believe," "in my experience," "mechanism-plausible, untested"), or (b) cut.

   A worked example of the three possible outcomes for a draft line:

   | Draft script line | Claim ledger entry | Verdict |
   |---|---|---|
   | "Habits take about two to five months to become automatic." | Habit-formation timeline → [Singh-2024] → T2 (general population) | **Keep**, with the population caveat spoken or captioned |
   | "Streaks keep you motivated for the long haul." | Gamification durability → [KimCastelli-2021] → effects decay, negative long-term | **Cut or invert** — the registry contradicts it |
   | "The Weekly Reset feels like a deep breath for your week." | No factual claim — experience | **Keep**, framed as experience ("for me…"), never as evidence |
3. **Foundation §4 check.** The claim ledger is verified line-by-line against [00 — Evidence Foundation](00-evidence-foundation.md): numbers match the registry exactly (effect sizes, n, populations, caveats); §6 rulings applied (e.g., timer widgets described as T3, reminders never described as standalone fixes); §5 "what the evidence does NOT show" scanned for accidental overclaim.
4. **Tier language check (§10, elaborated in [13 — Ethical Marketing Framework](13-ethical-marketing-framework.md)).** Marketing-language rules applied to educational copy too: no "clinically proven," no "rewires," no "dopamine hack," no outcome promises, no shame hooks. T2/T3 concepts must carry adjacent honesty ("studied in the general population, not yet in ADHD trials"; "community-endorsed, unproven"). The standing disclosure block (§9 below) is attached.
5. **Publish** with the platform's citation surface (per §5 below) and the correct pillar/tier tag in our content log.
6. **Corrections policy.** When we get something wrong: fix fast, visibly, without drama. (a) Pin a correction comment on the piece within 24 hours of confirming the error; (b) edit descriptions/captions where the platform allows; (c) log it on the blog's public **Corrections page** (date, error, fix, affected posts); (d) if the error is material to the concept taught, publish a follow-up piece — corrections are RE-pillar content and, done well, they *build* trust rather than spend it.

**Quarterly content audit.** Every quarter (aligned with the metrics ritual in §8): re-check the 20 highest-traffic pieces plus everything tagged T3 against the *current* foundation file. If the foundation was updated (new trial, revised ruling), affected content is corrected, annotated, or retired. Evergreen blog posts get a visible "last reviewed" date.

## 4. The myth-response library

Ten myths we actively counter, each with the exact honest framing we use on camera and in copy. Tone rules: correct the *idea*, never mock the *person* repeating it; always land on a hopeful, usable alternative; never invent a counter-statistic.

1. **"Habits form in 21 days."** What we say: "In the classic cohort study, automaticity took a **median of 66 days — with a range of 18 to 254** [Lally-2010]. A systematic review puts it at **2–5 months** [Singh-2024]. And here's the hopeful part the myth hides: missing a single day didn't materially harm habit formation [Lally-2010]. You're not failing; the timeline you were sold was fiction."
2. **"Brain-training games strengthen your ADHD brain."** What we say: "An adult ADHD trial found no significant benefit over active control [Stern-2016], and a meta-analysis of nine trials found no significant improvement in executive function, memory, or symptoms by outcome [Elbe-2023]. Neurofeedback did no better than sham [NimmoSmith-2020]. What has trial support is less flashy: structured external scaffolding and skills practice [Safren-2010][Solanto-2010]."
3. **"Just set reminders."** What we say: "A randomized trial added weekly reminder messages to an ADHD program — **no overall effect** on completion or practice [Nordby-2022]. Reminders only earn their keep when they point at one small, pre-decided action — 'if it's 8pm, I pay one bill' — which is the if-then planning pattern with meta-analytic support in the general population (d = 0.65) [Gollwitzer-2006]. A reminder is a doorbell; you still need to decide, in advance, who answers."
4. **"Budgeting apps are proven to work for ADHD."** What we say: "The money *problem* is well documented — more debt, less saving, more impulsive buying [Bangma-2019][Barkley-2008]. But **no trial has validated any budgeting tool or feature** for ADHD financial outcomes — and a review of 109 ADHD apps found none with proof of effectiveness [Pasarelu-2020]. That includes ours. We publish what each feature is based on, and we're building the measurement to test it."
5. **"Dopamine detoxes / dopamine hacks."** What we say: "We went looking for the outcome trials behind 'dopamine hacks.' They don't exist. Dopamine framing is a *mechanism story*, not evidence that a hack changes behavior — and we use mechanism language sparingly and label it as such. What's actually documented is a stronger pull toward immediate rewards (steeper delay discounting) [DelayDiscounting-Meta] — which is why we design structure around future tasks instead of promising brain chemistry tricks."
6. **"ADHD is a knowledge problem — you just need to learn better systems."** What we say: "Knowing isn't the bottleneck. In financial education research, teaching explains roughly **0.1% of the variance** in later behavior [Fernandes-2014]. ADHD support that works doesn't lecture — it *externalizes*: the calendar holds the memory, the checklist holds the sequence [Offloading-2025][Safren-2010]. That's Law 1 of everything we build: externalize, don't educate."
7. **"Streaks build discipline."** What we say: "Gamification effects collapse over time: about d = 1.57 in the first week, d = 0.30 by twenty weeks, and *negative* sustained over years [KimCastelli-2021]. Meanwhile, missing one day doesn't derail habit formation [Lally-2010]. A streak turns one bad day into a reason to quit. We track weekly momentum instead — that's why Anchor Routines has no streaks to lose."
8. **"An AI coach beats human support."** What we say: "In a randomized trial, chatbot-supported psychoeducation was **not superior** to a conventional app — both groups improved, no group-by-time advantage, no knowledge advantage [Selaskowski-2023]. One smaller mixed study exists [Jang-2021], but the honest summary is: AI is an unproven delivery layer (T3), not an active ingredient. We'll use it where it reduces friction, and we won't market it as a coach."
9. **"People abandon ADHD tools because they're lazy."** What we say: "In a trial of a self-guided ADHD program, only **29% completed all modules** — and the program still helped [Kenter-2023]. Another app was liked and adopted, yet engagement declined and outcomes didn't move [FOCUS-2023]. When most users 'fail' a tool, the tool failed the users. Abandonment is a design problem — which is why restarting (Comeback Mode) is a first-class feature for us, not an afterthought."
10. **"Body doubling is clinically proven."** What we say: "Body doubling is **community-endorsed and mechanism-plausible — and there are no randomized trials** [BodyDoubling-HCI]. Both halves matter. Unproven doesn't mean useless; it means unmeasured. We offer it as an experimental, optional practice, labeled exactly that way — and we'd love to see someone run the trial."

**Deploying the library.** Each myth exists in four pre-approved formats so responding never requires fresh research under time pressure: a 45–60s vertical script (TikTok/Shorts/Reels), an 8-slide carousel, a Threads post with source reply, and a standing blog section. The wording above is canonical; derivatives may shorten it but may not sharpen it beyond what the key supports. The library is a living document: myths get retired when the discourse moves on, and new entries only ship after passing §3 gates 2–4.

## 5. Per-platform strategy

**Standing note (foundation rule):** everything in this section about formats, cadences, and distribution mechanics is **current best practice to validate**, dated 2026-07. We do not claim knowledge of ranking algorithms, and we re-verify platform specs quarterly. No engagement statistic below is presented as a platform fact; internal targets are ours alone.

**The hook honesty test.** Curiosity is allowed; deception is not. Every hook on every platform must pass all five checks before it ships:

1. **Keyed:** any factual claim in the hook appears in the piece's claim ledger with a §4 key. A hook that can't be keyed can't run.
2. **Paid off early:** the hook's question is answered in the first third of the piece — no bait, no withheld answer, no "wait for it."
3. **Compression-safe:** the caveat survives the shortening. If the honest version is boring, choose a different hook — never a dishonest one.
4. **Clean of §10 violations:** no manufactured urgency or scarcity, no fear-bait, no shame ("stop being lazy" is banned as both a hook and a worldview).
5. **True in isolation:** a person who reads *only* the hook and scrolls on leaves with a true belief.

### 5.1 YouTube (long-form) — the trust engine

- **Funnel role:** the weekly seed asset everything else is cut from; where depth, nuance, and caveats live; the strongest trust-builder we have.
- **Native format:** 8–15 minute single-concept explainers; chapters; on-screen claim cards; a "Caveats" chapter in every research video; description carries the full reference list.
- **Honest hooks (titles/openers):**
  1. "109 ADHD apps. Zero with proof they work. Here's what *does* have evidence." [Pasarelu-2020]
  2. "What randomized trials actually found about adult ADHD planning skills" [Safren-2010][Solanto-2010]
  3. "Researchers added reminders to an ADHD program. Completion didn't budge." [Nordby-2022]
  4. "The 21-day habit myth: what the two best studies really measured" [Lally-2010][Singh-2024]
  5. "ADHD and money: what a 27-year follow-up found — and what nobody has tested yet" [Barkley-2008]
- **Citing research:** verbal cite + lower-third card (design, population, n, year, e.g., "RCT · 120 adults, self-reported ADHD · 2023"); description lists every source exactly as characterized in foundation §4 and links our public **Evidence page** (the blog mirror of the registry).
- **Solo cadence:** 1/week aspirational; 2/month is the honest minimum viable; never sacrifice gate 3–4 of §3 for schedule.
- **CTA policy:** education first — default CTA is the free First Action Kit; product mentions limited to PW-pillar videos and a one-line description mention elsewhere; every product mention names its evidence tier.
- **Risks:** solo-creator burnout (mitigated by §6); health-misinformation policy exposure (we are the compliant ones — keep disclaimers on); comment sections drifting into medical advice (moderation macros, §7).

### 5.2 YouTube Shorts — the sampler

- **Funnel role:** discovery; each Short is one claim card from the seed video, pointing back to the full context.
- **Native format:** under ~60 seconds, vertical; hook in the first line; one concept, one citation card; caption repeats the key claim.
- **Honest hooks:**
  1. "A trial sent people weekly reminders. Completion didn't move." [Nordby-2022]
  2. "Habits took a median of 66 days in the classic study — and one missed day didn't matter." [Lally-2010]
  3. "Six jam options beat twenty-four — 30% vs 3% bought. What that suggests about your to-do list." [IyengarLepper-2000]
  4. "Brain-training apps: nine randomized trials, no significant executive-function gains." [Elbe-2023]
  5. "'Clinically proven ADHD app'? A review of 109 found zero with proof." [Pasarelu-2020]
- **Citing research:** persistent corner citation card (≥3 seconds, readable at arm's length: "Meta-analysis · 9 RCTs · n=285"); pinned comment carries the full reference and the caveat sentence.
- **Solo cadence:** 2–3/week, all cut from the weekly seed in one editing block; zero net-new scripting.
- **CTA policy:** end-card to the full video; First Action Kit in pinned comment; no product pitches in Shorts.
- **Risks:** nuance loss at 60 seconds (rule: if the caveat doesn't fit, the claim doesn't ship as a Short); accidental overclaim via compression — gate 4 of §3 applies to captions too.

### 5.3 TikTok — reach and myth-response

- **Funnel role:** widest cold reach; primary home of the myth-response library (§4); talking to people who've only ever heard hype.
- **Native format:** 15–60s talking-head with text overlay; green-screen over the actual study first page/abstract; stitches responding to myths (respectfully — we correct ideas, not people).
- **Honest hooks:**
  1. "I went looking for the studies behind 'dopamine hacks.' Here's what I found." (finding: mechanism stories, no outcome trials — §6 ruling on mechanism language)
  2. "An AI ADHD coach went head-to-head with a plain app. Neither won." [Selaskowski-2023]
  3. "This meta-analysis found if-then plans hit d = 0.65. Here's how to write one in 20 seconds." [Gollwitzer-2006]
  4. "Only 29% finished this ADHD program — and it still worked. Let's talk about 'lazy.'" [Kenter-2023]
  5. "Body doubling: loved by the community, untested in trials. Both things are true." [BodyDoubling-HCI]
- **Citing research:** green-screen the source itself; on-screen key card; full citation + caveats in a pinned comment (link options are limited — the bio links the Evidence page).
- **Solo cadence:** 2–4/week, batched monthly from the myth library and seed cuts; never chase trends that require same-day filming.
- **CTA policy:** link-in-bio to First Action Kit; product content clearly labeled PW; no urgency or scarcity framing, ever (§10).
- **Risks:** the outrage gradient (myth-response can slide into dunking — tone rules in §4 are binding); trend audio pushing us into claims we can't key; platform moderation of health content; audience skew younger than our buyer — accept it, it serves the public-good mission.

### 5.4 Instagram — Reels, Carousels, Stories

- **Funnel role:** warmth and visual teaching; where the community sees the humans behind Anchor; carousel = the best save-able teaching format we have.
- **Native format:** Reels mirror TikTok edits; carousels are 8–10 slides, one concept arc, final slide always **Sources**; Stories are process, polls, and boundaried Q&A (no medical advice — macro in §7).
- **Honest hooks:**
  1. Carousel: "What adult-ADHD trials actually support — and 5 popular things they don't (yet)."
  2. Carousel: "Why every effective program includes a weekly review — and how The Weekly Reset works in 15 minutes." [Solanto-2010][NICE-NG87]
  3. Reel: "You missed a day. In the classic habit study, a single miss didn't materially hurt formation — here's the data." [Lally-2010]
  4. Carousel: "'Time blindness' is measurable. The gadgets that claim to fix it aren't proven. Here's the difference." [TimePerception-Review][Hallez-2024]
  5. Reel: "'Externalize, don't educate' — why offloading beats trying harder." [Offloading-2025]
- **Citing research:** final Sources slide with keys + one-line study descriptors; alt text carries citations for accessibility; Stories link-sticker to the Evidence page.
- **Solo cadence:** 2 Reels (repurposed) + 1 carousel/week (carousel is derived from the blog post, §6); Stories only when there's something real to show.
- **CTA policy:** First Action Kit and Anchor Lab; one PW post per week maximum; captions repeat evidence-tier labels on any product mention.
- **Risks:** the aesthetic trap (beautiful ≠ functional — the OR pillar exists to fight this); before/after culture (banned in §2); save-bait listicles that violate one-concept discipline.

### 5.5 Pinterest — evergreen search and printables

- **Funnel role:** slow, compounding search traffic to the blog and free printables; reaches planners-and-printables audiences competitors reach with false claims. Long content half-life is commonly reported for this platform — treat as a hypothesis to validate against our own analytics, not a fact.
- **Native format:** static pins (checklist previews, one-quote teaching cards), idea pins summarizing blog posts; every pin links to a blog post that carries full citations.
- **Honest hooks (pin titles):**
  1. "The 15-minute ADHD Weekly Reset — free printable checklist (built on trial-tested review rituals)" [Solanto-2010]
  2. "One next action: a task-breakdown worksheet from CBT methods studied in adults with ADHD" [Safren-2010]
  3. "Bill-day routine: an if-then plan template (the pattern behind d = 0.65 in general-population research)" [Gollwitzer-2006]
  4. "A habit tracker with no streaks to lose — momentum tracker printable" [KimCastelli-2021][Lally-2010]
  5. "ADHD paycheck checklist — honest about what's proven and what isn't" (§4D ruling)
- **Citing research:** pin descriptions carry a one-line evidence note; the destination blog post carries the full reference list — the pin never claims more than the post substantiates.
- **Solo cadence:** batch-design 5–10 pins monthly from existing assets; schedule weekly; near-zero marginal creative cost.
- **CTA policy:** pins lead to free value (printables, posts) which lead to the First Action Kit; no direct product pins outside clearly labeled PW pins.
- **Risks:** quote-card culture strips caveats (rule: no statistic pin without its caveat on the same image); our pins being re-captioned by others — watermark the Evidence page URL; keyword-stuffing temptation degrading honesty.

### 5.6 LinkedIn — the workplace lens

- **Funnel role:** credibility with professionals, employers, and clinicians; the WORK angle of the evidence; slowest but highest-trust cold channel for The External Brain.
- **Native format:** text posts (150–300 words, one claim, one caveat, one takeaway); document carousels (the Instagram carousel re-exported); occasional long article for RE-pillar deep dives.
- **Honest hooks:**
  1. "In a 23-study meta-analysis, psychosocial interventions moved work outcomes (d = 0.56) more than medication did in that domain (d = 0.19). Here's what that means for managers." [Lauder-2024]
  2. "Only 29% of users finished an ADHD program that worked. What that teaches anyone who ships software." [Kenter-2023]
  3. "Financial-literacy training explains ~0.1% of the variance in later financial behavior. Why teaching alone rarely changes behavior — and what does the heavy lifting." [Fernandes-2014]
  4. "We publish the evidence tier of every feature we ship. Here's the template, free." (Evidence Notes)
  5. "The cheapest accommodation nobody budgets for: a protected 15-minute weekly review." [Solanto-2010][NICE-NG87]
- **Citing research:** author-year inline plus study descriptor; link to the Evidence page (we test link placement empirically rather than following folklore about link penalties).
- **Solo cadence:** 1–2/week, written in the same batch as the newsletter; carousels are re-exports, not new work.
- **CTA policy:** newsletter-first; product mentions only in PW-labeled posts and in the profile; no lead-gen gimmicks ("comment PDF and I'll DM you" — no).
- **Risks:** hustle-culture drift (our anti-grind voice must hold); armchair-clinician replies under our posts (moderation §7); pressure to genericize into "productivity" content and lose the ADHD specificity.

### 5.7 Blog/SEO — the canonical reference layer

- **Funnel role:** the permanent, linkable home of every concept; hosts the public **Evidence page** (mirror of foundation §4), the Corrections page, and printables; captures search intent our honest titles can win.
- **Native format:** 1,500–2,500-word pillar posts, one per content pillar plus one per seed video; H2-scannable; claim ledger rendered as a visible reference list with tier labels; "last reviewed" date on every post.
- **Honest hooks (H1s / search targets):**
  1. "Do ADHD apps work? What the trials actually show" [Pasarelu-2020][attexis-2026][Kenter-2023][Inflow-2026]
  2. "ADHD and money: strong evidence for the problem, honest silence on solutions" [Bangma-2019][Barkley-2008]
  3. "How long do habits really take? 21 days vs. the data" [Lally-2010][Singh-2024]
  4. "Body doubling for ADHD: what we know, what we don't" [BodyDoubling-HCI]
  5. "The best ADHD planner is the one you can restart: a guide to forgiveness-first systems" [Lally-2010][KimCastelli-2021]
- **Citing research:** full academic-style reference list mapped 1:1 to foundation §4 keys; tier label beside each claim; caveats in-line, not in footnotes.
- **Solo cadence:** 2/month, produced by expanding the seed-video script (already claim-checked); the marginal cost is editing, not research.
- **CTA policy:** every post ends with The External Brain signup; PW posts may link products with Evidence Notes; no affiliate links inside RE-pillar posts (see §9).
- **Risks:** SEO gravity toward overclaiming titles ("best ADHD app that works") — we bid on honest phrasings instead; health-content search scrutiny (credentials transparency, §9, is our answer); AI-generated competitor sludge — differentiation is the visible claim ledger, which sludge can't fake.

### 5.8 The External Brain (newsletter) — the owned audience

- **Funnel role:** the asset we own outright; deepest trust; where launches happen; the only channel where we initiate contact — which is a responsibility. This section covers the weekly editorial issue; the full email program (welcome, onboarding, launch, retention, and reactivation sequences) is specified in [10 — Email Marketing](10-email-marketing.md).
- **Native format:** weekly; scannable in under 3 minutes by design (our readers have ADHD — respect working memory): **one concept → one next action → one honest evidence note ("The Receipts") → one small product or community note (optional)**. Plain layout, minimal images, no walls of text.
- **Honest hooks (subject lines):**
  1. "One next action (it's smaller than you think)"
  2. "The study that made us remove streaks" [KimCastelli-2021]
  3. "66 days, not 21" [Lally-2010]
  4. "Why we won't promise you savings" (§4D ruling)
  5. "Your backlog isn't a moral failing" [Kenter-2023]
- **Citing research:** "The Receipts" footer section — key, one-line study descriptor, one-line caveat, link to the Evidence page.
- **Solo cadence:** weekly without exception, but the exception-proof design is that a valid issue can be 5 lines (see Comeback plan, §6); consistency beats length.
- **CTA policy:** the most product-permissive channel, still capped: at most 1 product-forward issue in 4; every product mention carries its tier language; launch sequences never use countdown/scarcity theater (§10).
- **Risks:** contributing to inbox overwhelm for the exact audience we serve (mitigation: the 3-minute format and a proud, easy unsubscribe); deliverability hygiene; the temptation to over-mail during launches.

### 5.9 Threads — conversation and nuance

- **Funnel role:** real-time conversation, myth-response in dialogue form, and a listening post for what the community actually struggles with (feeds the Inbox).
- **Native format:** text-first single posts and short threads; screenshots of study abstracts with plain-language annotation; questions that invite experience-sharing, not engagement-farming.
- **Honest hooks:**
  1. "There's no head-to-head trial of any ADHD planner vs. a plain spreadsheet. Including ours. Thread on what evidence *does* exist." (§5 of the foundation)
  2. "Running list of things the ADHD internet treats as proven that are actually 'community-endorsed, untested.'" [BodyDoubling-HCI]
  3. "A reminder is not a plan. A plan is: 'If it's 8pm, I pay one bill.' (d = 0.65, general-population meta-analysis.)" [Gollwitzer-2006][Nordby-2022]
  4. "Today's Weekly Reset took 12 minutes and surfaced two bills. That's the whole system doing its one job." (process post — experience, not evidence)
  5. "Which ADHD 'fact' should we source-check next? We read the studies so you don't have to."
- **Citing research:** claim post + immediate self-reply with source screenshot and Evidence-page link; never let a claim post stand alone unsourced.
- **Solo cadence:** 3–5 posts/week drafted in the weekly batch; 15 minutes/day of genuine replies, timeboxed.
- **CTA policy:** newsletter only; no product pushes in conversational threads; it reads as spam and is spam.
- **Risks:** dunk-culture pull (quote-posting bad takes for sport — banned; we respond to *ideas* at their best); context collapse on nuanced claims; time-sink replying (the timebox is a hard rule).

> **Evidence:** platform portfolio and tactics are design rationale (untested as marketing science); the *content* carried on them is keyed T1–T3 per piece; cross-platform teaching format echoes multi-channel delivery evidence only loosely [Selaskowski-2022] · **Confidence:** Low (channel tactics), High (content accuracy controls) · **Rationale:** meet people where they already scroll, with the seed-and-waterfall model keeping a solo creator's load sublinear in channel count · **Expected outcome:** compounding email list growth from education-first funnels; qualified, expectation-calibrated buyers · **Downside:** nine channels risk spreading thin — the tier system in §6 exists so channels can be paused without the engine stopping · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 6. The repurposing waterfall and the ADHD creator's week

We are an ADHD creator serving ADHD audiences. The production system must survive bad weeks, because bad weeks are a design constraint, not a surprise (Law 7).

**The waterfall (one seed, nine surfaces):**

```
1 YouTube seed video (one concept, fully claim-checked)
   ├─→ 2–3 Shorts / TikToks / Reels (cut, not re-scripted)
   ├─→ 1 blog post (script expanded; claim ledger becomes the reference list)
   │      └─→ 1 newsletter issue (post distilled to concept + action + Receipts)
   │      └─→ 1 carousel (post compressed to 8–10 slides + Sources slide)
   │             └─→ LinkedIn document re-export
   └─→ 3–5 pins (checklist/quote cards linking to the blog post)
        + 3–5 Threads posts (claims + source replies, drafted from the ledger)
```

The claim check happens **once**, at the seed. Everything downstream inherits it. No derivative may add a claim the seed's ledger doesn't contain — compression may drop detail, never caveats.

**The weekly batch schedule (capture → batch → reset):**

| Day | Block (≤3 hrs) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Write | Pick next seed from The Inbox (pre-triaged); script + claim ledger (§3 gates 1–3) |
| Tue | Record | Batch-record: seed video + 2–3 vertical pieces in one setup (one setup, one teardown) |
| Wed | Edit/derive | Edit seed; cut verticals; draft blog from script |
| Thu | Distribute | Newsletter + carousel + pins + Threads drafts; schedule everything |
| Fri | Channel Weekly Reset | 15 minutes: what shipped, what's scheduled, one metric glance (§8), triage Inbox ideas, pick next week's seed. Then stop. |

Idea capture runs continuously into The Inbox (voice notes, comment screenshots, community questions) — nothing is evaluated at capture time, nothing is trusted to memory [Offloading-2025]. The channel's Weekly Reset is the same protected ritual we sell (Law 6), applied to ourselves.

**Sustainable minimum viable cadence (the floor, not the goal):** 1 seed video per 2 weeks + 1 weekly newsletter (even at 5 lines) + scheduled pins. Everything else is officially optional. The floor is chosen so it's achievable in ~4 focused hours/week.

**Comeback plan for missed weeks (Comeback Mode for the channel):** miss a week → no apology video, no explanation spiral, no double-posting to "make up for it." Protocol: (1) send the 5-line newsletter ("one concept, one action, see you next week"); (2) re-promote one evergreen seed; (3) resume the normal schedule as if nothing happened. The habit data strictly covers single missed occasions, not whole weeks [Lally-2010] — but the design translation (Law 7) is identical: recovery, not penance. The backlog of unposted ideas is archived to The Inbox, not owed to anyone.

**The content log (our single source of accountability):** every published piece gets one row — date, platform, pillar/tier tag, seed it derives from, claim-ledger link, CTA used, and correction status. The quarterly audit (§3, §8) runs off this log, not off memory [Offloading-2025]. One system, maintained during the Friday Weekly Reset, replaces every "did we already cover this?" decision.

**The first eight seeds (starter queue).** The engine needs a running start that exercises every part of this document — pillars, myth library, PW transparency, and the RE voice. The full one-year calendar lives in [12 — Editorial Calendar](12-editorial-calendar.md); these eight prove the format:

| Wk | Seed video | Pillar | Tier of concept taught | Keys |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What executive function actually is — and why "try harder" was never the plan | EF | T1 (package components) | [Safren-2010][Knouse-2017][Offloading-2025] |
| 2 | The 21-day habit myth: what the studies really measured | HA | T2 (general population) | [Lally-2010][Singh-2024] |
| 3 | One next action: task breakdown the way the trials taught it | PL | T1 | [Safren-2010][Solanto-2010][Matsumoto-2024] |
| 4 | Do ADHD apps work? The honest state of the evidence | RE | T1 packages; market claims unsupported | [attexis-2026][Kenter-2023][Inflow-2026][Pasarelu-2020] |
| 5 | Reminders alone don't work — the cue-to-action pattern that has evidence | PL | T2 with T1 constraints (Law 5) | [Nordby-2022][Gollwitzer-2006] |
| 6 | ADHD and money: the problem is proven; the tools are not | MO | Problem evidence (§4D ruling) | [Bangma-2019][Barkley-2008][Fernandes-2014] |

> **Evidence:** T2 — the schedule operationalizes capture/offloading [Offloading-2025], review rituals [Solanto-2010][NICE-NG87], forgiveness after misses [Lally-2010], and batch/default structure [Jachimowicz-2019]; its effectiveness *as a creator workflow* is untested design rationale · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** a production system with a low floor and a scripted restart survives the creator's own executive-function variance · **Expected outcome:** ≥80% of weeks hit the minimum viable cadence over a year, misses recover within one week · **Downside:** batching concentrates risk into single days; a lost Tuesday can cost the week — the floor cadence is the buffer · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Priority:** High

## 7. Community and comments

**Moderation policy (published, pinned, enforced):**

- **No medical advice — from us or from commenters.** We never advise on diagnosis, medication (starting, stopping, dosing), or supplements; comments doing so get the signpost macro: *"This is the kind of question a prescriber/clinician should answer for your specific situation — please talk to yours. Here's why we don't answer it here: [link to our disclosure page]."* Repeat medical-advice comments are removed.
- **Crisis-resource macro.** Any mention of self-harm or suicidality gets a warm, immediate template response pointing to crisis services (e.g., 988 in the US; local equivalents via findahelpline.com), plus encouragement to reach out to a person they trust — and no further engagement with the thread's content. Money-and-mental-health content (the [Swedish-Registry] findings) always ships with resources attached and is never used in hooks or thumbnails.
- **Kindness norms.** No shame language ("lazy," "excuses"), no diagnosis-policing ("you don't seem ADHD"), no unsolicited treatment evangelism. We correct ideas generously and people never.
- **Honesty norms extend to the community.** Commenters' miracle claims ("this cured me in a week") get gentle context, not amplification; testimonials are never screenshotted into marketing as outcome evidence (§10).

**The macro shelf** (pre-written so moderation never depends on that day's executive function — ours included):

- *Medical signpost* (above), *crisis response* (above), plus:
- *Myth correction:* "Totally understandable that you've heard that — it's everywhere. The best data we know of says [honest framing from §4, with key]. Full breakdown with sources here: [link]. And genuinely, thanks for raising it."
- *Not-a-clinician:* "We're tool-makers and educators, not clinicians — for medication or diagnosis questions, your prescriber/clinician is the right person, and it's worth the ask. What we *can* offer is the organization-side research: [Evidence page link]."
- *Warm boundary* (for shame-policing between commenters): "We don't do 'lazy' here — the completion data says abandonment is a design problem, not a character one [Kenter-2023]. Comment edited/removed per community rules."

**Engagement that doesn't exploit:** no rage bait, no fake controversy, no "comment X to trigger the algorithm" farming, no open loops we don't close, no posting designed to provoke correction. Questions we ask the community are questions we actually want answered — they feed The Inbox and Anchor Lab research, and we say so. Engagement is a cost we ask of people with attention differences; we spend it carefully (Law 10 applied to media).

## 8. Content metrics (Law 10, applied to media)

Platform engagement is never the goal; it is a *leading indicator* that teaching landed and trust grew. The goal remains the canonical §11 functional metrics for people who become users.

**Leading indicators we watch (per piece and per quarter):**

| Leading indicator | What it proxies | What it must never become |
|---|---|---|
| Saves + shares | "Worth keeping / worth giving away" — the two most teaching-aligned actions platforms expose | A save-bait format target ("save this so the algorithm…") |
| Completion / average view duration | Pacing and comprehension of the concept taught | A retention-editing arms race that crowds out caveats |
| Click-to-email rate (First Action Kit opt-ins per 1,000 views) | Earned trust; the funnel's honest heartbeat | Aggressive pop-up and gate tactics |
| Newsletter replies + sentiment | Real-life usefulness ("this helped me do X this week") | Reply-bait prompts and manufactured polls |
| Correction rate (errors caught post-publish per 50 pieces) | Health of the §3 accuracy workflow | A reason to stop correcting publicly (hiding errors ≠ fewer errors) |
| Myth echo-back (qualitative, sampled quarterly) | Concepts actually landing ("okay but it's 66 days, not 21") | Comment-fishing for validation |

**Explicit vanity non-goals:** follower counts, viral one-offs, raw view totals, engagement-rate theater, posting-streak length (we of all people will not keep a streak for its own sake), rage-driven comment volume. None of these appear in our quarterly review as success measures.

**Quarterly review ritual (one afternoon, calendared):** (1) leading indicators vs. last quarter; (2) content audit per §3 (top-20 traffic + all T3-tagged pieces re-checked against the current foundation); (3) myth library refresh — retire won arguments, add new myths from The Inbox; (4) platform portfolio check — pause the weakest channel guilt-free if the floor cadence was missed >20% of weeks; (5) one experiment chosen for next quarter, pre-registered in the content log with the metric it must move.

> **Evidence:** Governance per Law 10 [FOCUS-2023][NimmoSmith-2020][LopezCampos-2025]; the specific leading indicators are design rationale, untested · **Confidence:** Moderate · **Rationale:** engagement-as-cost framing keeps the media arm from optimizing toward the compulsive patterns the product arm explicitly rejects · **Expected outcome:** decisions driven by save/share/opt-in/reply quality rather than reach; quarterly kill-or-keep calls actually made · **Downside:** we will look "small" next to engagement-optimized competitors, and that must be acceptable to everyone involved · **Difficulty:** Low · **Priority:** High

## 9. Disclosure ethics

- **"I am not a clinician."** Standing disclosure in every video description, blog footer, newsletter footer, and link-in-bio page: *"We make self-help organization tools and educational content. We are not clinicians. Nothing here is medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and none of it replaces a clinician, medication, or therapy."* Spoken aloud in any video touching diagnosis, medication, or treatment decisions. This is the §10 mandatory disclosure, worn proudly rather than buried.
- **Sponsorships and affiliates:** clearly labeled at the top, always (plain "This is sponsored" / "affiliate link — we earn a commission"), following applicable disclosure law as a floor, not a ceiling. We only take sponsors/affiliates for things we actually use, and **never** from categories that trade on ADHD desperation: supplements/nootropics, "brain training" ([Stern-2016][Elbe-2023] — we will not sell what the evidence refutes), neurofeedback-at-home devices [NimmoSmith-2020], or any product marketed with "clinically proven" claims it cannot substantiate [Pasarelu-2020].
- **RE-pillar content is never sponsored.** Research explainers carry no sponsorships and no affiliate links — the trust thesis (§1) is worth more than any single deal, and a sponsored study-breakdown is a contradiction in terms.
- **Our own products are conflicts too.** PW content and any product mention states plainly that we sell the thing being shown, and shows its Evidence Notes tier on screen. We hold Anchor to the same standard we hold the 109 apps [Pasarelu-2020]: the problem is proven, our solutions are honestly labeled, and outcome claims wait for our own measurements (foundation Part 14).
- **When to platform actual clinicians:** any content that meaningfully concerns diagnosis, medication, therapy selection, comorbidities (depression, anxiety), or crisis brings on a credentialed guest (named credentials, shown on screen) or carries a named clinician's script review. Guest clinicians review their segments before publish; we do not edit their caveats out. Coaching-adjacent topics disclose the evidence level for coaching itself [ACCESS-2021][PrevattYelland-2015] — promising, not definitive, and studied mostly in college students.
- **Testimonials** are stories, labeled as stories — never presented as outcome evidence, never solicited with incentives, never edited to imply treatment effects (§10).
